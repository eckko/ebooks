Dhritarashtra said: O Sanjay, assembled in the holy land (धर्मक्षेत्र/pious place) of Kurukshetra, desiring to fight (युयुत्सवः/eager for battle), what did my sons and the sons of Pandu do?
Sanjay said: But then, seeing the army (अनीकम्/military force) of the Pandavas arranged in battle formation (व्यूढम्/strategic alignment), King Duryodhana approached the teacher (आचार्यम्/guru) and spoke these words.
O teacher, look at this mighty army (चमूम्/military force) of the sons of Pandu, arranged in formation (व्यूढाम्/marshaled) by your intelligent (धीमता/wise) disciple, the son of Drupada.
Here are heroes, great archers (महा इष्वासाः/mighty bowmen), equal to Bhima and Arjuna in battle (युधि/war), Yuyudhana, Virata, and the great chariot-warrior (महारथः/eminent warrior) Drupada.
Dhrishtaketu, Chekitan, and the powerful (वीर्यवान्/valiant) king of Kashi, Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Shaibya, the best among men (नरपुंगवः/excellent human being).
And Yudhamanyu the brave (विक्रान्तः/courageous), and the powerful (वीर्यवान्/mighty) Uttamauja, the son of Subhadra, and the sons of Draupadi, all indeed great chariot-warriors (महारथाः/elite fighters).
But O best of the twice-born (द्विजोत्तम/noble Brahmin), know those who are distinguished (विशिष्टाः/prominent) among us, the leaders (नायकाः/commanders) of my army, I name them for your information (संज्ञार्थम्/awareness).
Yourself, Bhishma, and Karna, and Kripa, victorious in battle (समितिञ्जयः/always winning wars), Ashvatthama, Vikarna, and also the son of Somadatta.
And many other heroes (शूराः/warriors) who have risked their lives (त्यक्त जीविताः/ready to die) for my sake, equipped with various weapons and missiles (नाना शस्त्र प्रहरणाः/diverse armaments), all experts in warfare (युद्ध विशारदाः/skilled in battle).
That army of ours protected (अभिरक्षितम्/guarded) by Bhishma is insufficient (अपर्याप्तम्/unlimited or inadequate), but this army of theirs protected by Bhima is sufficient (पर्याप्तम्/limited or adequate).
Therefore, stationed in your respective positions (अवस्थिताः/placed) at all the entry points (अयनेषु/strategic strategic passes), all of you must surely protect Bhishma alone.
To generate joy (हर्षम्/happiness) in him, the oldest of the Kuru clan, the glorious grandfather (पितामहः/babu), roared loudly like a lion (सिंह नादम्/war cry) and blew his conch shell (शङ्खम्/शंख).
Then conch shells, kettledrums (भेर्यः/large drums), cymbals (पणव/small drums), drums (आनक/beating instruments), and horns (गोमुखाः/cow-horn instruments) suddenly blared forth, and that sound became tumultuous (तुमुलः/uproarious/भयानक शोर).
Then, stationed in a magnificent chariot (स्यन्दने/chariot) yoked with white horses (श्वेतैः हयैः/white steeds), Madhava and the son of Pandu also blew their divine (दिव्यौ/celestial) conch shells.
Hrishikesha blew the Panchajanya, Dhananjaya blew the Devadatta, and the doer of wolf-like ferocious deeds (भीम कर्मा/terrible actor) Vrikodara blew the great conch Paundra.
King Yudhishthira, the son of Kunti, blew the Anantavijaya, while Nakula and Sahadeva blew the Sughosha and Manipushpaka.
And the king of Kashi, the supreme archer (परम इष्वासः/great bowman), and Shikhandi the great chariot-warrior (महारथः/mighty fighter), Dhrishtadyumna, Virata, and the unconquered (अपराजितः/invincible) Satyaki.
Drupada and the sons of Draupadi, all together O Lord of the Earth (पृथिवीपते/King), and the mighty-armed (महाबाहुः/strong-armed) son of Subhadra, blew their conch shells separately.
That roaring sound tore (व्यदारयत्/shattered) the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons, making both the sky (नभः/space) and the earth resonate with a tumultuous noise (तुमुलः अभ्यनुनादयन्/resounding roar).
Then, seeing the sons of Dhritarashtra positioned (व्यवस्थितान्/standing in order), the Pandava whose flag has the emblem of Hanuman (कपिध्वजः/monkey-bannered), when the discharge of weapons (शस्त्र सम्पाते/shooting of arrows) was about to begin, raised his bow, and then spoke these words to Hrishikesha, O Lord of the Earth (महीपते/King).
Arjun said: O Achyuta (अच्युत/infallible or immortal Lord), place my chariot (रथम्/chariot) in the middle between both the armies.
So that I may observe (निरीखे/behold) these men standing here desiring to fight (योद्धु कामान्/eager for battle), with whom I must fight in this enterprise of war (रण समुद्यमे/battle preparation).
I want to look at those who are going to fight (योत्स्यमानान्/ready to battle), who have assembled (समागताः/gathered) here, wishing to do what is pleasing (प्रिय चिकीर्षवः/desiring well-being) in battle to the evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra.
Sanjay said: O descendant of Bharata, Hrishikesha, being spoken to thus by Gudakesha, having stationed the excellent chariot (रथ उत्तमम्/best chariot) in the middle between both the armies.
In front of Bhishma, Drona, and all the rulers of the earth (महीक्षिताम्/kings), said: O Partha, behold these assembled (समवेतान्/gathered) Kurus.
There Partha saw standing uncles, grandfathers (पितामहान्/ancestors), teachers (आचार्यान्/gurus), maternal uncles (मातुलान्/maternal relatives), brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends as well.
Fathers-in-law (श्वशुरान्/in-laws) and well-wishers (सुहृदः/friends) in both the armies as well. Seeing all those kinsmen (बन्धून्/relatives) positioned there, the son of Kunti.
Overwhelmed by supreme compassion (कृपया परया आविष्टः/filled with intense pity), sorrowing (विषीदन्/depressed), spoke this. Arjun said: O Krishna, seeing this my own people (स्वजनम्/kinsmen) present here desiring to fight (युयुत्सुम्/eager for battle).
My limbs are failing (सीदन्ति/weakening) and my mouth is completely drying up, and a tremor (वेपथुः/shivering) is created in my body and my hair stands on end (रोमहर्षः/goosebumps).
The Gandiva bow slips (स्रंसते/drops) from my hand and my skin is burning all over, and I am not able to stand steady, and my mind is spinning as if (भ्रमति इव/reeling).
And I see adverse (विपरीतानि/unfavorable) omens (निमित्तानि/signs), O Keshava, and I do not foresee any good (श्रेयः/welfare) by killing my own kinsmen (स्वजनम्/family) in battle (आहवे/war).
I do not desire victory, O Krishna, nor kingdom, nor pleasures, what is the use of kingdom to us, O Govinda, or what is the use of enjoyments or even life?
They for whose sake kingdom, enjoyments, and pleasures are desired by us, those very persons are standing here in battle, having renounced their lives (प्राणान् त्यक्त्वा/giving up life) and wealth.
Teachers (आचार्याः/gurus), fathers, sons, and also grandfathers (पितामहाः/grandparents), maternal uncles, fathers-in-law, grandsons, brothers-in-law (श्यालाः/wife's brothers), and other relatives (सम्बन्धिनः/kinsmen).
These I do not wish to kill, even if they kill me, O Madhusudana, even for the sake of lordship over the three worlds (त्रैलोक्य राज्यस्य/dominion of three realms), then what to speak for the sake of the earth (मही कृते/for this land)?
By killing the sons of Dhritarashtra, what pleasure (प्रीतिः/happiness) can be ours, O Janardana? Sin (पापम्/vice) alone would take shelter in us by killing these aggressors (आततायिनः/terrorists or criminals).
Therefore, we are not justified (न अर्हाः/unworthy) to kill the sons of Dhritarashtra, who are our own relatives (स्वबान्धवान्/kinsmen). For how, by killing our own people (स्वजनम्/family), can we be happy (सुखिनः/peaceful), O Madhava?
Even if these people do not see, with their minds overpowered by greed (लोभ उपहत चेतसः/corrupted by avarice), the sin (दोषम्/fault) caused by the destruction of the family (कुल क्षय कृतम्/ruin of dynasty) and the crime in hostility towards friends (मित्र द्रोहे/betrayal of allies).
Why should it not be known by us, to turn away (निवर्तितुम्/refrain) from this sin (पापात्/evil), by us who clearly see the evil (दोषम्/fault) caused by the destruction of the family, O Janardana?
With the destruction of the family, the eternal (सनातनाः/ancient or timeless) family traditions (कुल धर्माः/dynastic duties) perish. When spirituality is destroyed, impiety (अधर्मः/righteousness lost) overcomes the entire family as well.
From the dominance of impiety, O Krishna, the women of the family become corrupted (प्रदुष्यन्ति/polluted). When women are corrupted, O descendant of Vrishni, there arises intermixture of castes (वर्ण सङ्करः/adulteration of lineage).
This mixture leads only to hell (नरकाय/damnation) for the destroyers of the family and for the family itself. Indeed, the ancestors (पितरः/forefathers) of these fall down, being deprived of the offerings of rice-balls and water (लुप्त पिण्ड उदक क्रियाः/ancestral rituals stopped).
By these misdeeds (दोषैः/vices) of the destroyers of the family, which cause the intermixture of castes (वर्ण सङ्कर कारकैः/lineage-mixing actions), the traditional caste duties (जाति धर्माः/community traditions) and the eternal family traditions are devastated (उत्साद्यन्ते/destroyed).
For those human beings whose family traditions are destroyed (उत्सन्न कुल धर्माणाम्/lost dynastic duties), O Janardana, living in hell (नरके/perdition) is assured (नियतम्/inevitable), thus we have heard from tradition.
Alas! We have resolved (व्यवसिताः/determined) to commit a great sin (महत् पापम्/grave crime), that we are prepared to kill our own kinsmen out of greed for the pleasure of kingdom (राज्य सुख लोभेन/desire for royal comfort).
If the weapon-handed (शस्त्र पाणयः/armed) sons of Dhritarashtra should kill me in battle while I am unresisting (अप्रतीकारम्/passive) and weaponless (अशस्त्रम्/unarmed), that would be better and safer (क्षेमतरम्/more beneficial) for me.
Sanjay said: Having spoken thus in the battlefield (सङ्ख्ये/war zone), Arjuna sat down on the seat of the chariot (रथ उपस्थे/chariot floor), casting away his bow along with arrows (स शरम् चापम्/arrows and bow), his mind overwhelmed with grief (शोक संविग्न मानसः/distressed heart).




Sanjaya said, to him who was thus overcome with pity (करुणा/दया), with eyes full of tears (अश्रु) and distressed, Madhusudana spoke these words.
The Supreme Lord said, from where has this delusion (मोह/कश्मलम्) come upon you in this time of crisis (विषम परिस्थिति/crisis), which is un-Aryan (अनार्य जुष्टम्/unworthy of a noble person), leading to non-heavenly realms (अस्वर्ग्यम्) and causing infamy (अपकीर्ति/disgrace), O Arjuna.
Do not yield to unmanliness (कायरता/impotence), O Partha, it does not befit you, casting off this petty weakness of heart (हृदय की दुर्बलता/faint-heartedness), arise, O scorcher of enemies.
Arjuna said, how can I fight with arrows (बाणों से/arrows) in battle against Bhishma and Drona, O Madhusudana, who are worthy of worship (पूजनीय/respectable), O destroyer of enemies.
It is better indeed to eat even begged food (भिक्षा का अन्न/begged food) in this world than to kill these great-souled gurus (महापुरुष गुरु/venerable teachers), by killing these gurus, who desire wealth and desires (अर्थ और काम/wealth-seeking), I would enjoy pleasures stained with blood (खून से सने भोग/blood-stained pleasures) in this very world.
We do not even know which is better for us (हमारे लिए श्रेष्ठ/better for us), whether we should conquer them or they should conquer us, those very sons of Dhritarashtra, after killing whom we do not wish to live (जीने की इच्छा/wish to live), are standing right in front of us.
My true nature is overpowered by the flaw of pity (कायरता का दोष/weakness of pity) and my mind is confused about duty (कर्तव्य विमूढ़/confused about righteousness), I ask you, tell me decisively what is good (कल्याणकारी/beneficial) for me, I am your disciple (शिष्य), please instruct me who has taken refuge in you (शरणागत/surrendered).
I do not see what can remove this sorrow (शोक/grief) of mine that is drying up my senses (इन्द्रियों को सुखाने वाला/drying the senses), even after obtaining an unrivalled (शत्रुहीन/unrivalled) and prosperous kingdom on earth or even lordship over the gods (देवताओं का आधिपत्य/lordship of gods).
Sanjaya said, having spoken thus to Hrishikesha, Gudakesha, the scorcher of enemies, said to Govinda, "I will not fight," and became silent (मौन/silent) indeed.
To him, O Bharata, Hrishikesha, smiling as it were (मुस्कुराते हुए/smilingly), spoke these words in the midst of both the armies (दोनों सेनाओं के बीच/midst of both armies) to the despondent one.
The Supreme Lord said, you grieve for those who are not to be grieved for (शोक न करने योग्य/unworthy of grief), yet you speak words of wisdom (बुद्धिमानी की बातें/wise words), the wise men (ज्ञानी लोग/learned pundits) do not grieve for the dead or for the living.
It is not that I did not exist at any time, nor you, nor these rulers of men (राजा लोग/kings), nor is it that we all shall cease to exist (अस्तित्व समाप्त होना/cease to exist) hereafter.
Just as the embodied soul experiences childhood (बाल्यावस्था/childhood), youth, and old age in this body, similarly it attains another body (दूसरा शरीर धारण करना/attainment of another body), the calm and wise person (धीर पुरुष/steady-minded) is not deluded by this.
The contacts of the senses with their objects, O son of Kunti, give winter and summer (शीत और उष्ण/cold and heat), happiness and distress (सुख और दुःख/pleasure and pain), they come and go and are temporary (क्षणिक/unsteady), endure them patiently (सहन करो/tolerate them), O Bharata.
The person whom these do not distress (व्याकुल करना/distress), O chief among men, who remains same in pain and pleasure (सुख-दुःख में समान/equal in pleasure and pain) and is wise, he is fit for immortality (मोक्ष/immortality).
The unreal has no existence (अस्तित्व/existence), and the real never ceases to be (अभाव न होना/non-existence), the ultimate truth of both has been seen by the seers of truth (तत्त्वदर्शी/seers of the ultimate reality).
Know that to be imperishable (अविनाशी/indestructible) by which all this universe is pervaded (व्याप्त/pervaded), no one can cause the destruction of this changeless soul (अव्यय/immutable).
These bodies of the eternal, indestructible, and immeasurable embodied soul (जीवात्मा/embodied soul) are said to have an end, therefore fight (युद्ध करो/fight), O Bharata.
He who thinks that this soul is a killer and he who thinks that it is killed, both of them do not know the truth (अज्ञानी हैं/do not know), this soul neither kills nor is killed.
This soul is never born nor does it die at any time, nor having come into being will it ever cease to be again, it is unborn, eternal (नित्य), evergreen, and ancient (पुरातन), it is not killed when the body is slain (शरीर के नष्ट होने पर/slaying of body).
He who knows this soul to be indestructible, eternal, unborn, and changeless (अव्यय/immutable), how and whom does that person, O Partha, cause to be killed, or whom does he kill.
Just as a person casts off worn-out clothes (पुराने वस्त्र/worn-out clothes) and puts on others that are new, similarly the embodied soul casts off worn-out bodies and enters into other new ones.
Weapons do not cut this soul (अस्त्र-शस्त्र/weapons), fire does not burn it, water does not wet it, and wind does not dry it.
This soul cannot be cut, cannot be burned, cannot be wetted, and cannot be dried indeed, it is eternal, all-pervading (सर्वव्यापी/all-pervading), stable, immovable, and ancient (सनातन/timeless).
This soul is said to be unmanifest (अव्यक्त/invisible), inconceivable (अचिन्त्य/beyond thought), and unchangeable (विकाररहित/immutable), therefore, knowing it as such, you should not grieve (शोक करना/lament).
And even if you think of this soul as constantly being born and constantly dying (मृत्यु होना/dying), even then, O mighty-armed one, you should not grieve like this.
For death is certain (ध्रुव/fixed) for one who is born, and birth is certain for one who is dead, therefore, over this unavoidable matter (अपरिहार्य विषय/inevitable matter), you should not grieve.
All beings are unmanifest in their origin (जन्म से पहले अप्रकट/unmanifest before birth), manifest in their middle state, O Bharata, and unmanifest again in their destruction (मृत्यु के बाद अप्रकट/unmanifest after death), what is there to lament about in this.
Someone sees this soul as a wonder (आश्चर्यवत्/marvel), and similarly another speaks of it as a wonder, and another hears of it as a wonder, yet even after hearing, no one knows it at all (कोई नहीं जान पाता/no one understands).
This embodied soul residing in the body of everyone, O Bharata, is eternally unslayable (वध न करने योग्य/invincible), therefore, you should not grieve for any living being (प्राणी/living beings).
Even considering your own duty (स्वधर्म/own righteousness), you should not waver, for there is nothing better for a Kshatriya than a righteous war (धर्मयुद्ध/war of righteousness).
Gained by chance (अपने आप प्राप्त/by its own accord) and opening the wide gate of heaven (स्वर्ग का द्वार/gateway to heaven), happy are the Kshatriyas, O Partha, who get such a battle.
But if you will not wage this righteous warfare (धर्मयुद्ध/righteous battle), then having abandoned your own duty and fame (कीर्ति/reputation), you will incur sin (पाप/sin).
And people will also speak of your infinite infamy (अकीर्ति/dishonour), and for a highly respected person (प्रतिष्ठित व्यक्ति/honoured person), dishonour is worse than death.
The great chariot-warriors (महारथी/great warriors) will think that you have retreated from battle out of fear (भय/fear), and you will look insignificant before those who used to hold you in high esteem (बहुत आदर देना/high esteem).
Your enemies (शत्रु/enemies) will speak many unspeakable words (अवांछनीय बातें/unspoken words), criticizing your capability (सामर्थ्य/ability), what could be more painful (दुःखदायी/distressing) than that.
Slain, you will attain heaven (स्वर्गलोक/heavenly realm), or conquering, you will enjoy the earth (पृथ्वी का सुख/earthly pleasures), therefore arise, O son of Kunti, resolved on battle (निश्चय करके/determined to fight).
Making pleasure and pain equal (समान समझना/treating as same), gain and loss, victory and defeat, then engage in battle (युद्ध के लिए तैयार हो जाओ/ready yourself for battle), and in this way you will not incur sin (पाप/sinful reaction).
This intellect (बुद्धि/understanding) has been declared to you in Sankhya philosophy, but now hear this regarding Yoga (कर्मयोग/path of selfless action), endowed with which intellect, O Partha, you will cast off the bondage of karma (कर्मों का बंधन/shackles of action).
In this path, there is no loss of effort (प्रयास का नाश/loss of effort) nor is there any reverse obstacle (कोई नुकसान या पाप/contrary production), even a little practice of this righteousness (धर्म/righteous duty) protects one from great fear (महान भय से रक्षा/saves from great danger).
In this path, O descendant of Kuru, the resolute intellect (दृढ़ निश्चय वाली बुद्धि/focused mind) is one-pointed, but the intellect of the irresolute (अस्थिर बुद्धि वाले/undecided people) is many-branched and endless indeed.
The unwise people (अज्ञानी लोग/ignorant persons) utter these flowery words (दिखावटी बातें/flowery speech), O Partha, being deeply attached to the letter of the Vedas (वेदों के बाहरी अर्थ में लीन/attached to Vedic rituals) and saying that nothing else exists.
Full of worldly desires, holding heaven as their supreme goal (स्वर्ग को ही श्रेष्ठ मानने वाले/aiming for heaven), their speech grants birth as the fruit of actions (जन्म और कर्म का फल/reward of birth and action) and is full of specific rituals for attaining enjoyment and power (भोग और ऐश्वर्य/pleasure and opulence).
For those who are deeply attached to pleasure and opulence (भोग-विलास में डूबे हुए/attached to luxury) and whose minds are carried away by that flowery speech, the resolute intellect (निश्चयात्मक बुद्धि/resolute mind) is not established in meditation (समाधि/steady concentration).
The Vedas deal with subjects of the three qualities of nature (तीन गुण - सत्त्व, रज, तम/three modes of nature), rise above these three qualities, O Arjuna, be free from dualities (द्वंद्वों से रहित/free from pairs of opposites), always established in pure goodness, independent of acquisition and preservation (योग-क्षेम से रहित/free from securing wealth), and centered in the self.
Whatever utility there is in a small well when there is a flood of water everywhere (सब तरफ पानी से भरा जलाशय/all-flooding water reservoir), that same utility exists in all the Vedas for a wise Brahmin who knows the ultimate truth (सत्य को जानने वाला/knower of absolute truth).
You have a right only to perform your duty (कर्म करने का अधिकार/right to perform action), but never to its fruits, let not the fruit of action be your motive (कर्मफल की इच्छा/motive for reward), nor let there be any attachment to inaction (कर्म न करने में आसक्ति/attachment to laziness).
Established in Yoga, perform your actions (अपने कर्तव्य कर्म करो/perform your duties), abandoning attachment, O Dhananjaya, and remaining balanced in success and failure (सफलता और असफलता में समान/even-minded in victory and loss), this evenness of mind (समता का भाव/evenness of mind) is called Yoga.
Indeed, mere action motivated by rewards is far inferior (बहुत निम्न श्रेणी का/far inferior) to the Yoga of intellect (बुद्धियोग/yoga of wisdom), O Dhananjaya, seek refuge in this divine intellect (बुद्धि की शरण लो/take shelter in wisdom), for those who seek fruits of actions are wretched (कृपण/misers).
One who is endowed with this wisdom casts off both good and bad deeds (पुण्य और पाप/virtue and vice) in this very life, therefore devote yourself to Yoga (कर्मयोग से जुड़ो/engage in yoga), for Yoga is skill in actions (कर्मों में कुशलता/skill in performance of work).
For the wise sages (ज्ञानी पुरुष/wise thinkers) endowed with wisdom, abandoning the fruit born of actions (कर्मों का फल त्यागकर/giving up rewards), and being completely freed from the bondage of birth (जन्म के बंधन से मुक्त/liberated from cycle of rebirth), attain that state which is free from all suffering (दुःख-रहित परम पद/painless state).
When your intellect crosses over the mire of delusion (मोह का दलदल/illusion's mire), then you will attain indifference (वैराग्य/detachment) towards what is yet to be heard and what has already been heard.
When your intellect, which is perplexed by hearing various scriptures (शास्त्रों को सुनकर भ्रमित/confused by conflicting views), remains unshakeable (निश्चल/unmoving) and steady in deep meditation (समाधि में स्थिर/fixed in trance), then you will achieve divine union (योग की प्राप्ति/realization of Yoga).
Arjuna said, what is the description (लक्षण/definition) of a person of steady wisdom (स्थिर बुद्धि वाला/man of steady wisdom) who is established in trance, O Keshava, how does that person of steady mind speak, how does he sit, and how does he walk.
The Supreme Lord said, when a person completely relinquishes all desires (सभी कामनाओं को त्यागना/gives up all desires) of the mind, O Partha, and is satisfied in the self by the self alone (अपने आप में संतुष्ट/satisfied within oneself), then he is said to be a man of steady wisdom.
One whose mind remains unagitated in miseries (दुःखों में व्याकुल न होने वाला/unperturbed in distress), who is free from longing in pleasures (सुखों की इच्छा से रहित/free from desire for pleasures), and from whom attachment, fear, and anger have departed (राग, भय और क्रोध से मुक्त/free from passion, fear, and anger), that sage is called a man of steady wisdom.
He who is without attachment everywhere (आसक्ति से रहित/free from attachment), and on meeting with good or evil (शुभ और अशुभ/fortunate or unfortunate events), neither rejoices nor hates (न प्रसन्न होना न द्वेष करना/neither praises nor dislikes), his wisdom is firmly established.
And when he completely withdraws his senses from the objects of the senses (इन्द्रियों को विषयों से समेटना/withdrawing senses from sense-objects), just as a tortoise draws its limbs into its shell from all sides, his wisdom is firmly established.
The objects of enjoyment fade away for the embodied soul who abstains from feeding on them (इन्द्रियों को आहार न देने वाला/abstaining from sensory enjoyment), but the taste or longing for them remains, even this longing vanishes upon seeing the Supreme Reality (परमात्मा का साक्षात्कार/realizing the Supreme).
Indeed, O son of Kunti, the turbulent senses (मंथन करने वाली इन्द्रियाँ/turbulent senses) of even a wise man who is practicing self-control (प्रयत्नशील ज्ञानी पुरुष/striving wise man) forcibly carry away his mind.
Restraining all of them, a disciplined person should sit focused on Me as the Supreme Goal (मेरे परायण होकर/devoted to Me), for one whose senses are under control (वश में होना/under complete mastery), his wisdom is firmly established.
While dwelling on the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment to them (आसक्ति/attachment), from attachment arises longing desire (काम/desire), and from desire stems anger (क्रोध/wrath).
From anger arises total delusion (भ्रम/delusion), from delusion comes confusion of memory (स्मृति का भ्रम/loss of memory), from confusion of memory comes destruction of intellect (बुद्धि का नाश/loss of reason), and from destruction of intellect, a person completely perishes (नष्ट हो जाना/ruined).
But a person of self-controlled mind, moving among sense objects with senses free from attachment and aversion (राग और द्वेष से मुक्त/free from liking and disliking) and brought under the control of the self, attains internal peace and grace (प्रसन्नता/serenity).
On attaining that divine grace, destruction of all his miseries (सब दुःखों का नाश/end of all sorrows) takes place, and the intellect of such a serene-minded person (प्रसन्न चित्त वाले का/peaceful-minded person) very quickly becomes fully stable.
There is no wisdom for an undisciplined person (योगरहित पुरुष/unconnected person), nor is there any meditation or right emotion for the undisciplined, and for one who has no peace of mind, where is the possibility of happiness (सुख/happiness).
For as the mind follows the roaming senses (भटकती हुई इन्द्रियाँ/wandering senses), it carries away the person's wisdom, just as a strong wind sweeps away a boat on the water (पानी में नाव/boat on water).
Therefore, O mighty-armed one, he whose senses are completely restrained from their respective sense objects (विषयों से रोक लेना/withdrawn from sense-objects) in every way, his wisdom is firmly established.
What is night for all living beings, in that state of self-control the disciplined sage keeps awake (जागृत रहना/remains awake), and that state in which all beings keep awake is night for the seeing sage (तत्त्वदर्शी मुनी/seer of truth).
Just as the ocean remains undisturbed and filled from all sides though waters enter it (नदियों का जल प्रवेश करना/waters entering), similarly he into whom all desires enter without causing disturbance attains peace, and not the desirer of desires (काम-कामी/one who chases desires).
The man who, abandoning all worldly desires (सभी इच्छाओं को छोड़कर/giving up all longings), moves about completely free from longing, devoid of possessiveness (ममता से रहित/free from 'mineness') and free from ego (अहंकार रहित/without ego), he attains ultimate peace.
This is the divine state (ब्रह्म को प्राप्त स्थिति/godly state), O Partha, having attained this, one is never deluded again, being established in this state even at the hour of death (अन्त काल में/at the end of life), one attains liberation in the Supreme Brahman (मोक्ष/supreme liberation).




Arjuna said: If intellect (बुद्धि/understanding) is considered by You as superior to action (कर्म/work), O Janardana, then why do You urge (नियोजित करना/force) me to this terrible action (घोर कर्म/dreadful war), O Keshava?
With this seemingly confusing (व्यामिश्र/perplexing) speech, You are bewildering (मोहित करना/clouding) my intellect, as it were; therefore, tell me definitely (निश्चित रूप से/with certainty) that one path by which I may attain the highest good (श्रेयस/ultimate benefit).
The Blessed Lord said: In this world, a twofold path (द्विविध निष्ठा/two types of faith) was previously stated by Me, O sinless one (अनघ/pure-hearted)—the path of knowledge (ज्ञान योग/philosophy) for the analytical thinkers (सांख्य/intellectuals) and the path of action (कर्म योग/selfless work) for the yogis (योगी/practitioners).
Not by merely abstaining from actions does a man attain freedom from reaction (नैष्कर्म्य/actionless state), nor does he attain perfection (सिद्धि/spiritual success) by renunciation (संन्यास/monastic life) alone.
Because, surely, no one can remain (तिष्ठति/stay) even for a moment without performing action (अकर्मकृत्/sitting idle); indeed, everyone is helplessly (अवशः/without control) driven to action by the qualities (गुण/modes of nature) born of primordial nature (प्रकृति/material energy).
He who restrains the organs of action (कर्मेन्द्रिय/physical senses) but sits revolving and remembering (स्मरन्/brooding over) the sense-objects (इन्द्रियार्थ/objects of enjoyment) in his mind, that self-deluded soul (विमूढात्मा/foolish person) is called a hypocrite (मिथ्याचार/pretender).
But he who controls the senses by the mind, O Arjuna, and begins the yoga of action (कर्म योग/path of selfless duty) through the organs of action without attachment (असक्त/detached), he is highly superior (विशिष्यते/excellent).
Perform your prescribed (नियत/obligatory) duty, for action is indeed better than inaction (अकर्म/doing nothing); moreover, even the maintenance of your body (शरीर यात्रा/physical survival) would not be possible (प्रसिद्धयेत्/accomplished) through inaction.
This world is bound by actions (कर्म बन्धन/bondage of work) except when action is performed for the sake of sacrifice (यज्ञ/offering to the Divine); therefore, O son of Kunti, perform your action for that purpose, free from attachment (मुक्त सङ्ग/without clinging).
Having created mankind along with sacrifice (यज्ञ/selfless duties) in the beginning, the Lord of creation (प्रजापति/Brahma) said: By this shall you multiply and prosper (प्रसविष्यध्वम्/increase population); let this be the granter of your desired wishes (कामधुक्/wish-fulfilling cow).
Nourish and please the gods (देवता/divine forces) with this, and let those gods nourish you; thus, mutually nourishing each other (परस्पर भावयन्तः/supporting one another), you shall attain the supreme good (परम् श्रेयः/highest welfare).
Nourished by sacrifice, the gods will surely give you the desired enjoyments (इष्ट भोग/pleasurable objects); he who enjoys these gifts given by them without offering to them in return is indeed a thief (स्तेन/robber).
The good people who eat the remnants of sacrifice (यज्ञ-शिष्ट/sanctified food) are freed from all sins (किल्बिष/faults); but those sinful ones (पापी/wicked people) who cook food only for their own sake (आत्म-कारणात्/selfish reasons) eat only sin (अघ/demerit).
Living beings are born from food (अन्न/grain), the production of food comes from rain (पर्जन्य/clouds), rain comes from sacrifice (यज्ञ/righteous acts), and sacrifice is born from prescribed action (कर्म/bounden duty).
Know that action originates from the Vedas (ब्रह्म/divine scriptural knowledge), and the Vedas originate from the Imperishable (अक्षर/the indestructible Supreme Being); therefore, the all-pervading divine reality (सर्वगत ब्रह्म/omnipresent Spirit) is eternally established in sacrifice (यज्ञ/selfless offering).
He who does not follow and rotate the wheel (चक्र/cosmic cycle) thus set in motion in this world, leads a sinful life (अघायुः/vicious existence) satisfying his senses (इन्द्रियाराम/sensual pleasure); he lives in vain (मोघम्/uselessly), O Partha.
But that man who rejoices only in the Self (आत्मरति/delighted in the soul), is fully satisfied with the Self (आत्मतृप्त/content within), and is completely content in the Self alone, for him there is no duty (कार्य/obligatory work) remaining to be done.
For him, there is no interest or gain (अर्थ/motive) whatsoever in this world by actions performed (कृतेन/done), nor any loss by actions not performed (अकृतेन/undone); nor does he have any dependence (व्यपाश्रय/reliance) for any purpose on any living being (सर्वभूतेषु/among all creatures).
Therefore, without attachment (असक्त/detached), always perform the action that needs to be done (कार्य कर्म/obligatory duty); for by performing action without attachment, a person attains the Supreme (परम्/the ultimate spiritual goal).
Indeed, Janaka and others attained perfection (संसिद्धि/highest success) through action alone; even for the purpose of global welfare and guidance of people (लोकसंग्रह/maintenance of the world order), you should perform your duty.
Whatever a great person (श्रेष्ठ/exemplary leader) does, that very thing common people (इतर जन/the rest of society) also do; whatever standard (प्रमाण/benchmark) he sets up, the world follows (अनुवर्तते/imitates) that.
There is nothing, O Partha, that must be done (कर्तव्य/compulsory duty) by Me in all the three worlds (त्रिभुवन/the triple realm), nor is there anything unattained to be attained (अवाप्तव्यम्/to be acquired); yet, I engage Myself (वर्ते/continue to act) in action.
For if I did not engage in action, ever untiring and alert (अतन्द्रितः/vigilant), O Partha, men would follow My path (वर्त्म/way) in every way (सर्वशः/completely).
These worlds would perish and fall into ruin (उत्सीदेयुः/be destroyed) if I should not perform action; I would be the cause of confused mixture of races (सङ्कर/social chaos) and would destroy (उपहन्याम्/ruin) these living beings.
As the ignorant people (अविद्वान्/unwise) act with attachment (सक्त/clinging) to action, O Bharata, so should the wise man (विद्वान/enlightened person) act without attachment, desiring to maintain world order (लोकसंग्रह/welfare of humanity).
The wise man should not create confusion or division in the intellect (बुद्धिभेद/mental distraction) of ignorant people who are attached to actions (कर्मसङ्गी/bound to rituals); rather, engaging in devotion, he should make them perform all actions while properly performing them himself.
All actions are performed in every way by the qualities (गुण/modes) of material nature (प्रकृति/cosmic energy); but one whose mind is deluded by egoism (अहङ्कार विमूढात्मा/arrogant self-conceit) thinks, "I am the doer (कर्ता/author of action)."
But he who knows the truth (तत्त्ववित्/knower of ultimate reality), O mighty-armed one, about the respective divisions of material qualities and actions (गुण-कर्म विभाग/classification of forces and duties), knowing that qualities interact among qualities (गुणा गुणेषु वर्तन्ते/senses move among sense-objects), does not become attached (सज्जते/entangled).
Those deluded by the qualities of nature (प्रकृति-गुण सम्मूढ/ignorant of reality) get attached to the functions of the qualities (गुण-कर्म/material actions); the knower of the whole truth (कृत्स्नवित्/perfectly wise) should not disturb or unsettle (विचालयेत्/agitate) those lazy people of imperfect knowledge (अकृत्स्नविद् मन्दान्/ignorant and slow-witted).
Renouncing all actions (संन्यस्य/dedicating work) unto Me with a mind fixed on the Supreme Self (अध्यात्म चेतसा/spiritual consciousness), free from desire (निराशीः/without expectations), free from selfishness (निर्ममः/without possessiveness), fight, delivered from mental fever (विगतज्वरः/free from anxiety).
Those men who constantly practice (अनुतिष्ठन्ति/follow) this teaching (मत/doctrine) of Mine, full of faith (श्रद्धावन्तः/devout) and free from cavil or envy (अनसूयन्तः/non-critical), they are also freed from the bondage of actions (कर्म/karmic reactions).
But those who, envying and carping at this (अभ्यसूयन्तः/finding fault), do not practice My teaching, know them to be completely deluded in all knowledge (सर्वज्ञान विमूढ/totally ignorant), ruined and devoid of discrimination (अचेतसः/mindless).
Even a wise person (ज्ञानवान्/man of knowledge) acts in accordance with his own nature (प्रकृति/inherent disposition); all living beings follow their nature; what can restraint (निग्रह/forceful suppression) achieve?
Attachment and aversion (राग-द्वेष/liking and disliking) are deeply seated in the object of each sense organ (इन्द्रियार्थ/sensory objects); one should not come under their control (वश/sway), for they are indeed his adversaries and obstacles (परिपन्थिनौ/highway robbers on the path).
Better is one's own duty (स्वधर्म/natural prescribed duty) though devoid of merit or imperfect (विगुण/deficient), than the duty of another well-performed (स्वनुष्ठित/perfectly executed); destruction in one's own duty is better (श्रेयस/beneficial); the duty of another is fraught with fear (भयावहः/dangerous).
Arjuna said: Then by what impelled or driven (प्रयुक्तः/urged), O Varshneya, does this man commit sin (पाप चरति/do wrong deeds), even against his own will (अनिच्छन्/unwillingly), as if constrained by force (बलात्/violently compelled)?
The Blessed Lord said: This is desire (काम/lust), this is anger (क्रोध/wrath), born of the quality of passion (रजोगुण/mode of mobility); it is all-devouring (महाशन/insatiable eater) and greatly sinful (महापाप्मा/highly wicked); know this to be the enemy (वैरी/foe) here in this world.
As fire is covered by smoke (धूम/vapour), as a mirror (आदर्श/looking glass) is covered by dust (मल/dirt), and as an embryo (गर्भ/fetus) is wrapped by the amnion or womb (उल्ब/membrane), so is this knowledge covered by that desire.
This knowledge is covered (आवृतम्/veiled), O son of Kunti, by this constant enemy of the wise (ज्ञानिनः नित्य वैरी/perpetual foe of the knower), which takes the form of desire (काम रूप/lustful shape) and is an insatiable fire (दुष्पूर अनल/unquenchable flame).
The senses, the mind, and the intellect (बुद्धि/understanding) are said to be its seat or dwelling place (अधिष्ठान/abode); covering knowledge through these, it completely deludes (विमोहयति/bewilders) the embodied soul (देही/the jiva).
Therefore, you should first control the senses (इन्द्रिय नियमन/restraining organs), O bull of the Bharata dynasty, and surely destroy this sinful thing (पाप्मानम्/the wicked destroyer), which ruins scriptural knowledge and spiritual realization (ज्ञान-विज्ञान नाशनम्/destroyer of wisdom and experience).
They say that the senses are superior (पर/sublime) to the body; the mind is superior to the senses; but the intellect is superior to the mind; and that which is even superior to the intellect is He (सः/the Supreme Self).
Thus knowing Him who is superior to the intellect (बुद्धि/understanding), and stabilizing and controlling the lower self by the Higher Self (संस्तभ्य आत्मानम् आत्मना/restraining the mind by pure intellect), conquer the enemy, O mighty-armed one, who is in the form of desire (काम रूप/lustful nature) and is extremely difficult to seize (दुरासदम्/hard to conquer).




The Supreme Lord said. I taught this imperishable (अविनाशी/never-decaying) yoga to Vivasvan. Vivasvan told it to Manu, and Manu spoke it to Ikshvaku. || 1 ||
Thus received through the lineage (परम्परा/chain of teachers), the royal sages (राजर्षि/king-sages) knew this. O scorcher of enemies, by the great lapse of time here, that yoga was lost. || 2 ||
That same ancient (पुरातन/very old) yoga has been told today to you by Me. Because you are My devotee (भक्त/follower) and friend, this supreme secret (रहस्य/hidden truth) is revealed. || 3 ||
Arjuna said. Your birth is recent (अपरम्/later), and the birth of Vivasvan was earlier (परम्/ancient). How should I understand this, that You taught him in the beginning? || 4 ||
The Supreme Lord said. Many births of Mine have passed, and yours too, O Arjuna. I know them all, but you do not know them, O destroyer of foes (परन्तप/enemy-scorcher). || 5 ||
Although I am unborn (अजः/birthless) and My soul is imperishable (अव्यय आत्मा/decayless soul), and also the Lord of all living beings (ईश्वरः/master of creatures), establishing My own nature (प्रकृति/cosmic energy), I manifest by My own mystic power (आत्ममायया/divine illusion). || 6 ||
Whenever there is a decline (ग्लानिः/decay) of righteousness (धर्म/cosmic order), O Bharata, and a rise of unrighteousness (अधर्म/chaos), then I manifest Myself. || 7 ||
For the protection (परित्राणाय/saving) of the good, and for the destruction (विनाशाय/ruin) of the evil-doers, to firmly establish righteousness (धर्म संस्थापन अर्थाय/restoring moral order), I manifest age after age (युगे युगे/in every era). || 8 ||
He who knows My divine (दिव्यम्/extraordinary) birth and action in truth (तत्त्वतः/reality), after leaving the body, does not attain rebirth (पुनः जन्म/reincarnation), he attains Me, O Arjuna. || 9 ||
Freed from attachment, fear, and anger (वीत राग भय क्रोधाः/passion-fear-rage free), fully absorbed in Me and taking refuge in Me, many purified by the penance of knowledge (ज्ञान तपसा/wisdom-austerity) have attained My nature (मद्भावम्/divine state). || 10 ||
In whatever way people surrender to Me, in that same way I reward them. Men follow My path (वर्त्म/way) from all sides, O Partha. || 11 ||
Desiring success (सिद्धिम्/perfection) in actions, people worship the deities (देवताः/gods) here. Because in the human world, success born of action comes quickly (क्षिप्रम्/fast). || 12 ||
The four-fold order of society (चातुर्वर्ण्यम्/four castes) was created by Me according to the divisions of qualities and actions (गुण कर्म विभागशः/attribute-work classification). Although I am its creator, know Me to be the non-doer (अकर्तारम्/non-agent) and changeless (अव्ययम्/immutable). || 13 ||
Actions do not taint Me (लिम्पन्ति/bind), nor do I have any desire for the fruit of action (कर्म फले स्पृहा/craving for rewards). He who knows Me thus is not bound by actions (कर्मभिः न बध्यते/not chained by deeds). || 14 ||
Knowing this, action was performed by ancient seekers of liberation (मुमुक्षुभिः/salvation-seekers) also. Therefore, you perform action only, as was done by the ancients in ancient times (पूर्वतरम् कृतम्/done in past eras). || 15 ||
What is action and what is inaction, even the wise (कवयः/intelligent thinkers) are confused here. I will explain that action to you, knowing which you will be liberated from evil (अशुभात्/misfortune). || 16 ||
For one should understand the nature of right action (कर्मणः/dutiful deed), and should understand forbidden action (विकर्मणः/wrong deed), and should understand inaction (अकर्मणः/non-action); deep is the path of action (गहना कर्मणः गतिः/mysterious way of work). || 17 ||
He who sees inaction in action, and action in inaction, he is wise among men, he is yogic (युक्तः/harmonized) and a performer of all actions (कृत्स्न कर्म कृत्/complete-work doer). || 18 ||
He whose all undertakings (समारम्भाः/endeavors) are free from desire and mental resolve (काम सङ्कल्प वर्जिताः/wish-intention free), whose actions are burnt by the fire of knowledge (ज्ञान अग्नि दग्ध कर्माणम्/wisdom-fire scorched deeds), the wise call him a sage (पण्डितम्/learned scholar). || 19 ||
Giving up attachment to the fruit of action (कर्म फल आसङ्गम्/reward-bondage), always satisfied, independent (निराश्रयः/without support), even though fully engaged in action, he does nothing at all. || 20 ||
Free from desires, with mind and self controlled (यत चित्त आत्मा/restrained heart-soul), having given up all possessions (त्यक्त सर्व परिग्रहः/abandoned ownership), performing action only for the body (शारीरम् केवलम् कर्म/physical-only work), he incurs no sin (किल्बिषम्/fault). || 21 ||
Satisfied with whatever comes by chance (यदृच्छा लाभ सन्तुष्टः/unasked-gain happy), beyond dualities (द्वन्द्व अतीतः/free from opposites), free from envy, same in success and failure (समः सिद्धौ असिद्धौ च/equal in victory-defeat), even after acting, he is not bound. || 22 ||
Of one whose attachment is gone, who is liberated, whose mind is established in knowledge (ज्ञान अवस्थित चेतसः/wisdom-anchored intellect), who acts for sacrifice (यज्ञाय/divine offering), his entire action melts away (प्रविलीयते/completely dissolves). || 23 ||
The offering is Brahman, the oblation is Brahman, poured by Brahman into the fire of Brahman. Brahman alone is to be attained by him who is absorbed in Brahman-action (ब्रह्म कर्म समाधिना/divine-work meditation). || 24 ||
Some yogis offer sacrifice to the gods alone (दैवम् एव यज्ञम्/deity-worship offering), while others offer sacrifice by sacrifice itself into the fire of Brahman (ब्रह्म अग्नौ/divine fire). || 25 ||
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint (संयम अग्निषु/control fires); others offer sound and other sense objects into the fires of the senses (इन्द्रिय अग्निषु/sense fires). || 26 ||
Others offer all functions of the senses (इन्द्रिय कर्माणि/sensory activities) and functions of the vital breath (प्राण कर्माणि/breath actions) into the fire of the yoga of self-restraint (आत्म संयम योग अग्नौ/self-control yoga fire) lighted by knowledge. || 27 ||
Some perform sacrifice with material wealth (द्रव्य यज्ञाः/charity offerings), some with austerity (तपः यज्ञाः/ascetic offerings), some with yoga, and others of strict vows (संशित व्रताः/rigid promises) perform sacrifice of scriptural study and knowledge. || 28 ||
Others offer incoming breath into outgoing breath, and outgoing breath into incoming breath, blocking the paths of both breaths (प्राण अपान गती रुद्ध्वा/inhale-exhale movement stopping), absorbed in breath-control (प्राणायाम परायणाः/breath-regulation devoted). || 29 ||
Others, restricting their food (नियत आहाराः/controlled diet), offer vital breaths into vital breaths. All these are knowers of sacrifice (यज्ञ विदः/worship experts), whose sins are destroyed by sacrifice (यज्ञ क्षपित कल्मषाः/offering-cleansed faults). || 30 ||
Eating the remnants of sacrifice, which is like nectar (यज्ञ शिष्ट अमृत भुजः/offering-remainder nectar eaters), they go to the eternal Brahman. This world is not for the non-sacrificer, how then any other, O best of the Kurus? || 31 ||
Thus many kinds of sacrifices are spread out before the face of Brahman. Know them all to be born of action (कर्मजान्/deed-born); knowing this, you will be liberated (विमोक्ष्यसे/set free). || 32 ||
Better than sacrifice made of material objects is the sacrifice of knowledge (ज्ञान यज्ञः/wisdom offering), O scorcher of foes. All action in its entirety (अखिलम्/complete), O Partha, culminates in knowledge (ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते/ends in wisdom). || 33 ||
Know that by prostration (प्रणिपातेन/bowing down), by inquiry (परिप्रश्नेन/questioning), and by service. The wise, who have seen the truth (तत्त्व दर्शिनः/reality seers), will instruct you in knowledge. || 34 ||
Knowing which, you will not fall into delusion (मोहम्/illusion) again like this, O Pandava; and by this, you will see all living beings without exception in yourself, and then in Me. || 35 ||
Even if you are the most sinful (पापकृत्तमः/worst sinner) among all sinners, you will cross over all wickedness by the boat of knowledge alone (ज्ञान प्लवेन एव/wisdom-boat only). || 36 ||
As a blazing fire (समिद्धः अग्निः/ignited fuel) reduces fuel to ashes, O Arjuna, in the same way, the fire of knowledge (ज्ञान अग्निः/wisdom fire) reduces all actions to ashes. || 37 ||
There is no purifier (पवि॒त्रम्/cleansing agent) here equal to knowledge. A person perfected in yoga (योग संस्रीद्धः/yoga-matured seeker) finds that himself in due time within his own soul. || 38 ||
The man of faith gains knowledge (श्रद्धावान् लभते ज्ञानम्/faithful gets wisdom), being devoted to it and having mastered his senses (संयत इन्द्रियः/controlled organs). Having gained knowledge, he attains supreme peace (पराम् शान्तम्/highest peace) without delay. || 39 ||
The ignorant, the faithless (अश्रद्दधानः/doubting person), and the doubting soul goes to ruin (विनश्यति/perishes). For the doubting soul (संशय आत्मनः/skeptic self), there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness. || 40 ||
He who has renounced actions through yoga (योग संन्यस्त कर्माणम्/yoga-renounced deeds), whose doubts are torn apart by knowledge (ज्ञान सञ्छिन्न संशयम्/wisdom-slashed doubts), and who is self-possessed, actions do not bind him, O Dhananjaya. || 41 ||
Therefore, cutting this doubt born of ignorance (अज्ञान सम्भूतम्/ignorance-born) dwelling in your heart with the sword of knowledge (ज्ञान असिना/wisdom-sword) of the self, take refuge in yoga and stand up, O Bharata. || 42 ||




Arjuna said: Oh Krishna, you praise the renunciation (संन्यास/renouncing actions) of actions, and again you praise yoga (कर्मयोग/path of action); tell me definitely (निश्चित रूप से/with certainty) which one is the better (श्रेयस्कर/more beneficial) of these two.
The Blessed Lord said: Renunciation (कर्म संन्यास/giving up work) and karma yoga (निःस्वार्थ कर्म/selfless action) both lead to supreme liberation (मोक्ष/salvation); but of the two, karma yoga is superior (बेहतर/better) to the renunciation of actions.
He should be known as a constant renunciant (नित्य संन्यासी/perpetual ascetic) who neither hates nor desires; because, Oh mighty-armed one, one who is free from dualities (द्वंद्व रहित/free from opposites like joy and sorrow) is easily liberated from bondage (बंधन से मुक्त/freed from attachments).
Ignorant children (नासमझ लोग/unwise people) speak of Sankhya (ज्ञान मार्ग/path of knowledge) and Yoga (कर्म मार्ग/path of action) as separate, but the wise (विद्वान लोग/learned scholars) do not say so; one who is well-established (सही तरीके से स्थित/properly grounded) in even one, obtains the fruit (फल/result) of both.
The state (स्थान/status) which is obtained by the followers of Sankhya (ज्ञान योगी/philosophers) is also reached by the followers of Yoga (कर्म योगी/action-oriented people); he truly sees, who sees Sankhya and Yoga as one (एक समान/identical).
But renunciation, Oh mighty-armed one, is difficult to attain without yoga (निष्काम कर्म/action without desire); the meditative sage (मननशील मुनि/wise seeker) united with yoga reaches Brahman (परमात्मा/supreme reality) without delay (शीघ्र ही/very soon).
One who is united with yoga, pure in soul (शुद्ध अंतःकरण वाला/pure-hearted), controller of the self (आत्म-नियंत्रित/self-controlled), and master of the senses (जितेन्द्रिय/conqueror of senses), whose self becomes the self of all living beings (सभी जीवों की आत्मा/soul of all creatures), is not tainted (लिप्त नहीं होता/not entangled) even while performing actions.
The knower of truth (तत्वज्ञानी/realized person), being united with yoga, should think "I do nothing at all", while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, moving, sleeping, and breathing.
While speaking, releasing (त्याग करना/evacuating), grasping (ग्रहण करना/holding), opening and closing the eyes; holding the conviction (यह धारणा रखना/believing) that only the senses are operating among the sense objects (इंद्रियों के विषय/objects of perception).
He who performs actions by placing all actions in Brahman (परमात्मा में समर्पित/offering to God) and abandoning attachment (आसक्ति छोड़कर/giving up clinging), is not stained by sin (पाप से अलिप्त/affected by sin), just as a lotus leaf is not wet by water.
Yogis perform actions only with the body, mind, intellect, and even senses, abandoning attachment (आसक्ति का त्याग/detachment), for the purification of the self (आत्म-शुद्धि/cleansing the soul).
The united one (युक्त पुरुष/disciplined person), abandoning the fruit of action (कर्मफल का त्याग/giving up rewards), attains everlasting peace (परम शांति/ultimate peace); the un-united one (अयुक्त पुरुष/uncontrolled person), driven by desire (कामना के कारण/impelled by desire), being attached to the fruit, gets bound (बंधन में बंधना/entrapped).
The self-controlled soul (इंद्रियों को वश में करने वाला/master of senses), mentally renouncing all actions (मन से सब कर्म त्यागकर/rationally detaching), dwells happily in the city of nine gates (नौ द्वारों वाला शरीर/nine-gated body), neither acting nor causing actions to be done.
The Lord does not create agency (कर्तापन/doership) nor actions for the world, nor the connection with the fruits of actions (कर्मफल का संयोग/association with results); but nature (स्वभाव/inherent nature) alone operates.
The omnipresent Lord (सर्वव्यापी परमात्मा/all-pervading God) takes neither the sin nor the virtue (पुण्य/good deeds) of anyone; knowledge is covered by ignorance (अज्ञान से ढका हुआ/shrouded by delusion), by that all living beings are deluded (मोहित होना/confused).
But for those whose ignorance is destroyed by knowledge of the self (आत्मज्ञान/soul realization), their knowledge, like the sun (सूर्य के समान/resembling the sun), illuminates that Supreme Reality (परम तत्व/ultimate truth).
Those whose intellect is absorbed in That, whose self is identical with That, who are established in That, and whose supreme goal is That, go to the stage of non-return (मोक्ष/no rebirth), their sins being washed away by knowledge (ज्ञान से पवित्र/cleansed by wisdom).
The wise look with an equal eye (समदर्शी/impartial view) on a Brahmin endowed with learning and humility, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and even an outcast (चांडाल/dog-eater).
Even here on earth, creation (संसार/birth and death) is conquered by those whose mind is established in equality (समता में स्थित/equanimity); for Brahman is flawless and equal (निर्दोष और समान/faultless and balanced), therefore they are established in Brahman.
One should not rejoice on obtaining what is pleasant (प्रिय वस्तु/pleasant thing), nor get agitated on obtaining what is unpleasant (अप्रिय वस्तु/unpleasant thing); being firm in intellect (स्थिर बुद्धि/steady-minded), undeluded (मोह रहित/free from confusion), the knower of Brahman is established in Brahman.
The soul unattached to external touches (बाहरी स्पर्श/external contacts) finds that happiness which is in the self; he, with his soul united in Brahman-yoga (परमात्मा में लीन/absorbed in God), enjoys imperishable happiness (अक्षय सुख/eternal bliss).
For the enjoyments born of contact (इंद्रिय भोग/sensual pleasures) are only the wombs of misery (दुःख के कारण/sources of pain), having a beginning and an end, Oh son of Kunti; the wise man (बुद्धिमान व्यक्ति/enlightened person) does not rejoice in them.
He who is able to withstand (सहन करना/endure) here on earth, before liberation from the body (शरीर त्यागने से पहले/before death), the impulse arising from desire and anger (काम और क्रोध का वेग/force of lust and wrath), he is a yogi, he is a happy man.
He who has internal happiness (अंतरात्मा का सुख/inner joy), internal recreation (अंतरात्मा का आराम/inner rejoice), and similarly internal light (अंतरात्मा की ज्योति/inner illumination); that yogi, becoming Brahman (ब्रह्म स्वरूप/divine being), attains the absolute liberation of Brahman (ब्रह्म निर्वाण/supreme liberation).
The seers (ऋषिगण/holy sages) whose sins are destroyed (नष्ट पाप/cleansed of sins), whose doubts are torn apart (संशय दूर होना/dispelled dualities), who are self-controlled (आत्म-संयमी/disciplined souls), and devoted to the welfare of all beings (सबके कल्याण में लीन/engaged in good of all), attain the absolute liberation of Brahman.
To the ascetics (संन्यासी लोग/striving seekers) who are free from desire and anger, whose minds are controlled (नियंत्रित चित्त वाले/restrained minds), and who have realized the self (आत्मज्ञानी/soul-conscious), the absolute liberation of Brahman exists everywhere (सब ओर/all around).
Shutting out all external contacts (बाहरी विषयों को बाहर रखकर/excluding outer sense objects), fixing the gaze between the two eyebrows (भ्रूभंग के बीच/between the brows), and making equal the inward and outward breaths (प्राण और अपान वायु/prana and apana breaths) moving within the nostrils.
The meditative sage (मननशील मुनि/silent philosopher) whose senses, mind, and intellect are controlled, who is dedicated to liberation (मोक्ष परायण/aiming for salvation), and free from desire, fear, and anger, is indeed forever liberated (सदा मुक्त/always free).
Knowing Me as the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities (यज्ञ और तप का भोगी/consumer of rituals and penances), the Great Lord of all worlds (सब लोकों का मालिक/supreme ruler of all universes), and the friend of all living beings (सब जीवों का मित्र/well-wisher of all creatures), he attains peace (परम शांति/ultimate tranquility).




The Supreme Lord said, he who performs his bounden duty (कर्तव्य कर्म) without depending on the fruit of action (कर्मफल) is a sannyasi (संन्यासी/renunciant) and a yogi (योगी), and not the one who merely gives up the sacred fire (अग्नि त्याग करना) or remains without action (कर्महीन).
O Pandava, know that which they call sannyasa (संन्यास/renunciation) to be the same as yoga (योग); for no one can become a yogi without renouncing selfish desires (संकल्पों का त्याग करना).
For the sage (मुनि) who wishes to attain yoga, action (कर्म) is said to be the means (कारण); but for the same person who has attained yoga, tranquility (शमः/मन की शांति) is said to be the cause.
When a person is truly not attached (आसक्ति न होना) to the objects of the senses (इन्द्रियों के भोग) or to actions, and has renounced all selfish desires (सर्व संकल्प संन्यासी), then he is said to have attained yoga (योगारूढ़).
One should elevate oneself (आत्म-उद्धार करना) by one's own mind, and not degrade oneself (अपने आप को गिराना); for the mind alone is the friend (बन्धु) of oneself, and the mind alone is the enemy (शत्रु) of oneself.
The mind is the friend of that self by whom the self has been conquered (मन को जीतना); but for one who has not conquered the self (अनात्मनः), the mind behaves like an enemy in open hostility (शत्रुता/शत्रुवत्).
For one who has conquered the mind (जितात्मा) and is perfectly peaceful (प्रशान्त), the Supreme Soul (परमात्मा) is always deeply absorbed (समाहित); alike in cold and heat, pleasure and pain (सुख और दुःख), and also in honor and dishonor (मान और अपमान).
The yogi whose mind is fully satisfied with knowledge and wisdom (ज्ञान और विज्ञान से तृप्त), who is unshakeable (कूटस्थ/अचल), and who has conquered the senses (जितेन्द्रिय), is said to be united in yoga (युक्त); to him, a lump of earth, a stone, and gold are all equal (समदर्शी).
He excels (विशिष्यते/सबसे श्रेष्ठ है) who regards with an equal mind (सम बुद्धि) well-wishers, friends, enemies, the indifferent, mediators, the hateful, relatives (बन्धु), and also the righteous (साधु) and the sinners (पापी).
A yogi should constantly engage (सतत अभ्यास करना) his mind in meditation, staying alone in a secluded place (एकान्त स्थान), with mind and body controlled (संयमित चित्त), free from desires (निराशीः), and free from possessions (अपरिग्रह/संग्रह न करना).
Having established a firm seat (स्थिर आसन) for oneself in a clean place (शुद्ध देश), which is neither too high (अति उच्छ्रित) nor too low (अति नीच), and covered with cloth, deer-skin, and kusha grass (कुश) one over the other.
Sitting on that seat, making the mind one-pointed (एकाग्र मन) and controlling the actions of the mind and senses (चित्त और इन्द्रियों की क्रियाएँ), one should practice yoga for the purification of the self (आत्म-विशुद्धि).
Holding the body, head, and neck erect (समम्), motionless, and steady (स्थिर); and gazing fixedly at the tip of one's own nose (नासिकाग्र), without looking around in any direction (दिशाओं को न देखना).
With a completely peaceful mind (प्रशान्त आत्मा), free from fear (भयरहित), firm in the vow of celibacy (ब्रह्मचर्य व्रत), controlling the mind (मनः संयम्य), thinking of Me, and sitting united in yoga, holding Me as the supreme goal (मत्परः/सर्वोच्च लक्ष्य).
Thus constantly keeping the mind absorbed in meditation (सदा योग का अभ्यास करना), the yogi of controlled mind (नियतमानसः) attains the peace (शान्ति) which culminates in absolute liberation (निर्वाण) and which abides in Me.
Yoga is not possible (योग सिद्ध नहीं होता) for one who eats too much (अतिभोजी), nor for one who does not eat at all (व्रत रखने वाला); nor is it for one who sleeps too much (अति निद्रालु), nor for one who always stays awake (जागने वाला), O Arjuna.
For one who is regulated in diet and recreation (युक्त आहार और विहार), who is balanced in performing actions (कर्मों में संतुलित चेष्टा), and who is regulated in sleep and wakefulness (संतुलित सोना और जागना), yoga becomes the destroyer of all sorrows (दुःखहा/दुःखों का नाश करने वाला).
When the perfectly controlled mind (विनियत चित्त) rests steadily in the Self alone (आत्मनिष्ठ होना), free from longing (निःस्पृहः) for all desires, then he is said to be united in yoga (युक्त).
As a lamp in a windless place (हवा रहित स्थान) does not flicker (हिलना-डुलना नहीं), that is the simile (उपमा) remembered for a yogi of controlled mind, who is practicing meditation on the Self (आत्म-योग).
When the mind becomes quiet (उपरमते/शान्त होना), restrained by the practice of yoga (योग के अभ्यास से निरुद्ध), and when beholding the Self by the pure self, one remains satisfied within the Self (आत्म-संतोष).
When he feels that boundless joy (आत्यन्तिक सुख) which is grasped by the intellect (बुद्धि ग्राह्य) but is beyond the senses (इन्द्रियातीत); established in which, he never moves away (विचलित न होना) from the ultimate truth (तत्त्वतः).
Having gained which, he considers no other gain (लाभ) to be greater than that; and established in which, he is not shaken (विचलित नहीं होता) even by heavy sorrow (गुरुणा दुःखेन/बड़ा दुःख).
Let that state of severance from union with pain (दुःख के संयोग का वियोग) be known by the name of yoga (योग); this yoga must be practiced with firm determination (निश्चय) and with an un-depressed heart (अनिर्विण्ण चेतसा/बिना हताशा के).
Abandoning completely and without exception (अशेषतः) all desires born of mental speculations (संकल्प से उत्पन्न कामनाएँ), and completely restraining the entire group of senses (इन्द्रिय समूह) from all sides by the mind alone.
Gradually, step by step (शनैः शनैः), one should attain tranquility (उपरमेत्/शान्ति पाना) through the intellect sustained by fortitude (धैर्ययुक्त बुद्धि); fixing the mind steadily on the Self (आत्म-संस्थ), one should not think of anything else (कुछ और न सोचना).
From whatever cause the restless and unsteady mind (चंचल और अस्थिर मन) wanders away (भटकना), from that very cause one should restrain it (नियमन करना) and bring it back under the control of the Self alone (आत्म-वश करना).
Supreme happiness (उत्तम सुख) truly comes to this yogi whose mind is perfectly peaceful (प्रशान्त मन), whose passion is calmed (शान्त रजस/रजोगुण से रहित), who has become one with Brahman (ब्रह्मभूत), and who is free from sin (पापरहित).
Thus constantly engaging the self in yoga, the yogi, freed from all sins (पापमुक्त), easily enjoys the infinite bliss (अत्यन्त सुख) of contact with the Supreme Brahman (ब्रह्म-संस्पर्श).
The soul united in yoga (योगयुक्तात्मा) sees the Self abiding in all beings (सर्वभूतस्थ) and all beings in the Self; he looks upon everything with an equal eye (समदर्शन/सबको समान देखना).
He who sees Me everywhere (सर्वत्र) and sees everything in Me, I am never lost to him (परोक्ष न होना), nor is he ever lost to Me.
He who worships Me (भजन करना) as abiding in all beings, established in oneness (एकत्व भाव), that yogi dwells in Me (मुझमें वास करता है), no matter how he acts in his daily life.
He who, by comparison with himself (आत्म-औपम्येन), sees equality everywhere, O Arjuna, whether it be pleasure or pain (सुख या दुःख), that yogi is considered the highest (परम योगी).
Arjuna said, this yoga which has been described by You as equanimity (साम्यभाव/समानता), O Madhusudana, I do not see its steady continuation (स्थिर स्थिति) because of the restlessness of the mind (मन की चंचलता).
For the mind is restless (चंचल), O Krishna, turbulent (प्रमाथि/मंथन करने वाला), powerful (बलवान्), and obstinate (दृढ़); I consider its control (निग्रह) to be as difficult to achieve as controlling the wind (वायु को रोकना).
The Supreme Lord said, without doubt (असंशयम्), O mighty-armed one (महाबाहो), the mind is difficult to control (दुर्निग्रह) and restless (चलम्); but by practice (अभ्यास), O son of Kunti, and by detachment (वैराग्य), it can be restrained (वश में होना).
Yoga is difficult to attain (दुष्प्राप) by one of uncontrolled mind (असंयतात्मा)—this is My opinion (मतिः); but it can be achieved by one of controlled mind (वश्यात्मा) who strives through proper means (उपायपूर्वक प्रयत्न करना).
Arjuna said, he who has faith but lacks self-control (अयतिः/प्रयत्नहीन), whose mind wanders away from yoga (योग से विचलित मन), failing to attain perfection in yoga (योग संसिद्धि), what destination (गति) does he meet, O Krishna?
Does he not, fallen from both (उभय विभ्रष्ट/ज्ञान और कर्म दोनों से भटका), perish like a scattered cloud (छिन्न-भिन्न बादल), supportless (अप्रतिष्ठः), O mighty-armed one, and deluded on the path of Brahman (ब्रह्म मार्ग में विमूढ)?
You should completely dispel (छेदन करना/नष्ट करना) this doubt of mine, O Krishna; for there is no one else found besides You who can remove this doubt (संशय का नाश करने वाला).
The Supreme Lord said, O Partha, neither in this world (इहलोक) nor in the next world (परलोक) is there destruction (विनाश) for him; for anyone who performs auspicious deeds (कल्याणकृत्/अच्छा काम करने वाला), My dear friend (तात), never goes to a bad destination (दुर्गति).
Having attained the worlds of the righteous (पुण्यवानों के लोक) and having lived there for countless years (शाश्वतीः समाः), the one fallen from yoga (योगभ्रष्ट) is reborn in the house of the pure and the wealthy (पवित्र और श्रीमानों के घर).
Or he is born into a family of wise yogis (धीमतां योगिनां कुल); such a birth (जन्म) as this is indeed very rare to obtain (दुर्लभतर) in this world.
There he regains that spiritual intellect (बुद्धि संयोग) belonging to his previous body (पूर्व जन्म की बुद्धि), and he strives (यतते) harder than before for perfection (संसिद्धि), O descendant of Kuru.
By that very previous practice (पूर्वाभ्यास), he is carried forward helplessly (विवश होकर भी खिंचा चला जाना); even a seeker after knowledge of yoga (योग का जिज्ञासु) transcends the ritualistic words of the Vedas (शब्दब्रह्म से परे जाना).
But the yogi who strives with assiduous effort (प्रयत्नपूर्वक प्रयास करना), purified from all sins (संशुद्ध किल्बिषः) and perfected through many births (अनेक जन्मों में सिद्ध), then attains the supreme goal (पराम गतिम्/परम पद).
A yogi is superior to the ascetics (तपस्वियों से श्रेष्ठ), and is considered superior even to the men of knowledge (ज्ञानियों से भी श्रेष्ठ); he is also superior to the ritualistic workers (कर्मियों से श्रेष्ठ), therefore, O Arjuna, become a yogi (योगी बनो).
And of all yogis, he who worships Me with faith (श्रद्धावान्), with his inner self absorbed in Me (मद्गत अन्तरात्मा), he is considered by Me to be the most devoutly united in yoga (युक्ततमः/सर्वश्रेष्ठ योगी).




The Supreme Lord said: Hear how you, O Parth, with your mind attached (आसक्त/fully focused) to Me, practicing yoga (योग अभ्यास/spiritual discipline) and taking refuge (शरण/shelter) in Me, shall know Me completely without any doubt (असंशयम्/संशय रहित).
I shall declare to you this knowledge (ज्ञान/theoretical wisdom) along with realization (सविज्ञानम्/अनुभव सहित ज्ञान) completely, after knowing which nothing else remains (अवशिष्यते/बचता है) to be known in this world.
Among thousands of men, scarcely someone strives (यतति/प्रयास करता है) for perfection (सिद्धये/सफलता के लिए); and even among those striving who achieve perfection, scarcely someone knows Me in truth (तत्त्वतः/वास्तविक रूप में).
Earth, water, fire (अनलः/अग्नि), air, ether (खम्/आकाश), mind, intellect (बुद्धिः/समझ), and ego (अहङ्कारः/मैं-पन)—this is My divided nature (प्रकृतिः/माया) of eight kinds (अष्टधा/आठ प्रकार की).
This is My lower nature (अपरा प्रकृति/भौतिक शक्ति), but know My other higher nature (पराम प्रकृति/आध्यात्मिक शक्ति), O mighty-armed one, which is the life-force (जीव भूताम्/चेतन शक्ति) by which this world is sustained (धार्यते/संभाला जाता है).
Understand that all living beings (भूतानि/जीव) have these two as their source (योनीनि/उत्पत्ति स्थान); I am the source of origin (प्रभवः/उत्पत्ति) and also the dissolution (प्रलयः/विनाश) of the entire universe.
There is nothing else superior (परतरम्/श्रेष्ठ) to Me, O Dhananjaya; all this universe is strung (प्रोतम्/पिरोया हुआ) on Me, like pearls (मणि गणाः/मनके) on a thread (सूत्रे/धागे में).
I am the taste (रसः/स्वाद) in water, O son of Kunti, I am the light (प्रभा/चमक) of the moon and the sun, the sacred syllable Om (प्रणवः/ओंकार) in all the Vedas, sound in ether (खे/आकाश), and manhood (पौरुषम्/पुरुषत्व) in men.
I am the pure fragrance (पुण्यः गन्धः/पवित्र सुगंध) in the earth and the brilliance (तेजः/चमक) in fire (विभावसौ/अग्नि); I am the life (जीवनम्/प्राण) in all beings and the penance (तपः/तपस्या) in ascetics (तपस्विषु/तपस्वी लोगों में).
Know Me, O Parth, as the eternal seed (बीजम् सनातनम्/सदा रहने वाला कारण) of all beings; I am the intellect (बुद्धिः/समझ) of the intelligent, and the splendor (तेजः/प्रभाव) of the splendid (तेजस्विनाम्/तेजस्वी लोगों का).
I am the strength (बलम्/ताकत) of the strong, devoid of desire (काम/कामना) and attachment (राग/आसक्ति); and in all beings, I am desire (कामः/कामवासना) that is not opposed to righteousness (धर्म अविरुद्धः/धर्म के अनुकूल), O leader of the Bharatas.
Whatever states of being there are, whether harmonious (सात्त्विकाः/सतोगुणी), passionate (राजसाः/रजोगुणी), or ignorant (तामसाः/तमोगुणी), know that they proceed from Me alone; but I am not in them, they are in Me (तेषु ते मयि/वे मुझमें हैं).
Deluded (मोहितम्/अज्ञान में फंसा हुआ) by these three states consisting of qualities (गुणमयैः भावैः/प्रकृति के गुणों के भावों से), this entire world does not recognize Me, who am above them and imperishable (अव्ययम्/अविनाशी).
Indeed, this divine (दैवी/अलौकिक) illusion of Mine consisting of the three qualities is difficult to cross over (दुरत्यया/पार करना कठिन); those who surrender (प्रपद्यन्ते/शरण में आते हैं) to Me alone, cross over this illusion (मायाम् तरन्ति/माया को पार कर जाते हैं).
The evildoers (दुष्कृतिनः/पापी लोग), the foolish (मूढाः/मूर्ख), and the lowest of men (नराधमाः/नीच मनुष्य) do not surrender to Me; their knowledge is stolen (अपहृत ज्ञानाः/ज्ञान नष्ट हो जाना) by illusion, and they adopt the demoniac nature (आसुरम् भावम्/राक्षसी स्वभाव).
Four kinds of virtuous men (सुकृतिनः/पुण्य कर्म करने वाले) worship Me, O Arjun—the distressed (आर्तः/दुःखी), the seeker of knowledge (जिज्ञासुः/जानने की इच्छा रखने वाला), the seeker of wealth (अर्थार्थी/धन चाहने वाला), and the wise (ज्ञानी/आत्मज्ञानी), O leader of the Bharatas.
Among them, the wise man, who is constantly united (नित्य युक्तः/सदा मुझमें स्थित) and single-minded in devotion (एक भक्तिः/अनन्य भक्त), is the best (विशिष्यते/श्रेष्ठ है); for I am excessively dear (अत्यर्थम् प्रियः/बहुत प्रिय) to the wise man, and he is dear to Me.
Noble (उदाराः/महान) indeed are all these, but the wise man is My very self (आत्मा एव/मेरा ही स्वरूप)—this is My opinion (मतम्/विचार); for he, with a steadfast mind (युक्त आत्मा/मुझमें लगी हुई आत्मा वाला), is established in Me alone as the supreme goal (अनुत्तमाम् गतिम्/सर्वोत्तम गति).
At the end of many births (बहूनाम् जन्मनाम् अन्ते/अनेक जन्मों के बाद), the man of wisdom surrenders (प्रपद्यन्ते/शरण में आता है) to Me, realizing that Vasudeva is everything; such a great soul (महात्मा/श्रेष्ठ आत्मा) is very rare (सुदुर्लभः/कठिनाई से मिलने वाला).
Those whose knowledge has been swept away (हृत ज्ञानाः/बुद्धि भ्रष्ट हो जाना) by various desires (कामैः तैः तैः/उन-उन कामनाओं द्वारा) surrender to other demigods (अन्य देवताः/अन्य देवी-देवता), following particular rituals (नियमम् आस्थाय/नियमों को अपनाकर), impelled by their own nature (प्रकृत्या नियताः/स्वभाव के वश में होकर).
Whichever devotee (भक्तः/उपासक) desires to worship (अर्चितुम् इच्छति/पूजा करना चाहता है) with faith (श्रद्धया/विश्वास से) whatever form of deity, I make that faith of his steady (अचलाम् श्रद्धाम्/अटल विश्वास) and unwavering.
Endowed with that faith (श्रद्धया युक्तः/विश्वास से युक्त होकर), he engages in the worship (आराधनम् ईहते/पूजा करने का प्रयास करता है) of that deity and obtains from it his desires, which are in fact ordained (विहितान्/तय किए गए) by Me alone.
But the fruit (फलम्/परिणाम) obtained by those men of small intellect (अल्पमेधसाम्/कम बुद्धि वाले लोग) is temporary (अन्तवत्/नष्ट होने वाला); the worshipers of gods go to the gods, but My devotees (मद्भक्ताः/मेरे भक्त) come to Me also.
The undiscerning men (अबुद्धयः/बुद्धिहीन लोग) think of Me, the unmanifest (अव्यक्तम्/जो प्रकट न हो), as having come into a human form (व्यक्तिम् आपन्नम्/मनुष्य रूप में आना), not knowing My supreme nature (परम् भावम्/श्रेष्ठ स्वरूप) which is imperishable and ultimate (अनुत्तमम्/सर्वश्रेष्ठ).
I am not manifest (प्रकाशः/प्रकट) to everyone, being veiled by My creative illusion (योगमाया समावृतः/योगमाया से ढका हुआ); this deluded world (मूढः लोकः/अज्ञानी संसार) does not recognize Me, the unborn (अजम्/जन्मरहित) and imperishable.
I know the past (समतीतानि/बीते हुए), the present (वर्तमानानी/जो अभी चल रहा है), and the future (भविष्याणि/आने वाले) beings, O Arjun; but no one knows Me (माम तु वेद न कश्चन/मुझे तो कोई नहीं जानता).
By the delusion of dualities (द्वन्द्व मोहेन/सुख-दुःख आदि के भ्रम से) arising from desire and aversion (इच्छाद्वेष समुत्थेन/राग और द्वेष से पैदा होने वाले), O descendant of Bharata, all living beings fall into complete delusion (सम्मोहम् यान्ति/मोह को प्राप्त होते हैं) at birth, O scorcher of foes.
But those men of virtuous deeds (पुण्य कर्मणाम्/पवित्र कार्य करने वाले) whose sin has come to an end (अन्तगतम् पापम्/पाप नष्ट हो गया है), being freed from the delusion of dualities (द्वन्द्व मोह निर्मुक्ताः/द्वंद्व के भ्रम से मुक्त), worship Me with firm resolve (दृढ व्रताः/दृढ़ निश्चय वाले).
Those who strive (यतन्ति/प्रयास करते हैं) for liberation from old age and death (जरा मरण मोक्षाय/बुढ़ापे और मृत्यु से मुक्ति के लिए) by taking refuge in Me, they know that Brahman completely, and they know the supreme self (अध्यात्मम्/आत्म-ज्ञान) and all action (कर्म अखिलम्/सम्पूर्ण कर्म).
Those who know Me along with the material sphere (साधिभूत/भौतिक जगत सहित), the divine sphere (अधिदैवम्/देवताओं सहित), and the sacrificial sphere (साधियज्ञम्/यज्ञों सहित), they, with a steadfast mind (युक्त चेतसः/मुझमें एकाग्र चित्त वाले), know Me even at the time of death (प्रयाण काले अपी/अन्तिम समय में भी).




Arjuna said: What is that Brahman (परम सत्य/Supreme Reality), what is Adhyatma (अध्यात्म/inner self), and what is Karma (कर्म/action), O Purushottama (पुरुषोत्तम/Supreme Person)? What is called Adhibhuta (अधिभूत/physical manifestation), and what is termed Adhidaiva (अधिदैव/divine cosmic power)?
Who is Adhiyajna (अधियज्ञ/Lord of sacrifice), and how does He reside here in this body, O Madhusudana (मधुसूदन/destroyer of demon Madhu)? And how are You to be known at the time of departure (प्रयाण काल/death) by those who are self-controlled (नियत आत्मभिः/disciplined minds)?
The Supreme Lord said: The indestructible (अक्षरम्/imperishable) is the supreme Brahman, and its inherent nature (स्वभावः/essential nature) is called Adhyatma. The creative force that causes the birth and growth of living beings is named Karma, which signifies sacrifice (विसर्गः/offering).
Adhibhuta consists of the perishable manifestation (क्षरः भावः/changing material nature), and the cosmic soul is Adhidaivata (अधिदैवत/divine lord). I alone am the Adhiyajna here in this body, O best of the embodied beings (देहभृताम् वर/best among humans).
And at the final hour (अन्त काले/time of death), he who leaves his body (कलेवरम्/physical body) remembering Me alone, goes forth and attains My state of being (मद्भावम्/divine nature); there is no doubt (संशयः/skepticism) about this.
Whatever state of being (भावम्/nature or object) a person remembers when he leaves the body at the end, to that very state he goes, O son of Kunti, being always absorbed in that thought (तत् भाव भावितः/deeply engrossed).
Therefore, at all times, remember Me and fight; with your mind (मनः/intellect) and intellect (बुद्धिः/reasoning) dedicated to Me, you will surely reach Me without any doubt (असंशयः/unquestionably).
With a mind disciplined by the practice of meditation (अभ्यास योग युक्तेन/constant spiritual practice) and not wandering after anything else (न अन्य गामिना/one-pointed focus), a person reaches the divine Supreme Person (परमम् पुरुषम् दिव्यम्/Almighty God), O Partha, by constantly meditating on Him.
He who meditates on the all-wise (कविम्/seer), ancient, the ruler (अनुशासितारम्/controller), subtler than the subtlest (अणोः अणीयंसम्/smaller than an atom), the sustainer of everything (सर्वस्य धातारम्/supporter of all), of inconceivable form (अचिन्त्य रूपम्/beyond imagination), luminous like the sun, and beyond darkness (तमसः परस्तात्/ignorance).
At the time of departure with a steady mind (मनसा अचलेन/unshaken intellect), full of devotion (भक्त्या युक्तः/with reverence) and by the strength of yoga (योग बलेन/meditative power), properly placing the life-breath (प्राणम्/vital life force) between the eyebrows, he reaches that divine Supreme Person.
That imperishable state which the knowers of the Vedas speak of, which the self-controlled ascetics (यतयः/sages) free from attachment (वीत रागाः/passionless) enter, and desiring which they practice celibacy (ब्रह्मचर्यम्/sacred vow of continence), that goal (पदम्/state) I will briefly explain to you.
Controlling all the gateways of the senses (सर्व द्वाराणि/body openings), locking up the mind within the heart (हृदि निरुध्य/restraining in the heart), and fixing one's life-breath in the head, established in the practice of concentration (योग धारणाम्/steady yogic meditation).
Uttering the single-syllable Brahman 'Om' (एकाक्षरम् ब्रह्म/one-lettered cosmic sound), remembering Me as he departs and leaves the body, he reaches the supreme destination (पराम् गतिम्/highest spiritual goal).
For one who always remembers Me continuously with undivided mind (अनन्यचेताः/single-minded attention), I am easily attainable (सुलभः/easily accessible), O Partha, for that ever-integrated yogi (नित्य युक्तस्य/constantly connected mystic).
Having reached Me, the great souls (महात्मानः/noble beings) do not take rebirth (पुनः जन्म/reincarnation) in this temporary world full of misery (दुःख आलयम्/abode of sorrows), having attained the ultimate perfection (संसिद्धिम् पराम्/highest success).
From the realm of Brahma downwards, all worlds are subject to return (पुनः आवर्तिनः/repeated cycle of rebirth), O Arjuna; but having reached Me, O son of Kunti, there is no rebirth (पुनः जन्म न विद्यते/no reincarnation).
Those who know that a day of Brahma lasts for a thousand ages (सहस्र युग पर्यन्तम्/four billion three hundred million years) and that his night also ends in a thousand ages, they are the knowers of day and night (अहोरात्र विदः/people who understand cosmic time).
From the unmanifested state (अव्यक्तात्/invisible source) all manifested beings proceed at the coming of day; at the approach of night, they dissolve right there into that which is called the unmanifested (अव्यक्त संज्ञके/invisible cause).
This same multitude of living beings (भूत ग्रामः/multitude of creatures), coming into existence again and again, dissolves helplessly (अवशः/without control), O Partha, at the approach of night, and streams forth again at the coming of day.
But beyond this unmanifested nature, there is another eternal unmanifested reality (अव्यक्तः सनातनः/eternal invisible being) which, when all living beings perish, does not perish (न विनश्यति/is not destroyed).
This unmanifested reality is called the imperishable (अक्षरः/indestructible), and they call it the supreme goal; having attained it, souls never return (न निवर्तन्ते/do not come back), and that is My supreme abode (परमम् धाम/highest dwelling place).
That Supreme Person, O Partha, is attainable only by unswerving devotion (भक्त्या अनन्यया/exclusive love), within whom all living beings dwell and by whom all this universe is pervaded (ततम्/spread or covered).
Now I shall declare to you, O chief of the Bharatas, the times (काले/periods) departing in which yogis attain the state of non-return (अनावृत्तिमम्/no rebirth) and also the state of return (आवृत्तिम्/rebirth).
Fire, light, daytime, the bright fortnight, the six months of the northern path of the sun (उत्तरायणम्/sun's northern course); departing then, people who know Brahman go to Brahman.
Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern path of the sun (दक्षिणायनम्/sun's southern course); departing then, the yogi attains the lunar light (चान्द्रमसम् ज्योतिः/radiance of moon) and returns.
These two paths, the bright and the dark (शुक्ल कृष्णे/light and dark paths), are considered eternal in this world; by one, a person goes to non-return, and by the other, he returns again (आवर्तते पुनः/recycles into rebirth).
Knowing these two paths (सृती/paths), O Partha, no yogi is ever deluded (मुह्यति/confused); therefore, at all times, be steadfast in yoga (योग युक्तः/united in meditation), O Arjuna.
Whatever meritorious fruit (पुण्य फलम्/reward of righteous deeds) is promised for the study of Vedas, performance of sacrifices, austerities (तपःसु/penances), and charities, the yogi goes beyond all this by knowing this secret, and attains the supreme primeval state (परमम् स्थानम् आद्यम्/highest ancient abode).




The Supreme Lord said: But to you, who do not cavil (अनसूयवे/jealousy-free), I shall declare this most secret knowledge (गुह्यतमम्/deepest secret) along with realized wisdom, knowing which you will be released from evil (अशुभात्/misfortune or suffering).
This is the king of sciences (राजविद्या/supreme knowledge), the king of secrets (राजगुह्यम्/royal secret), the supreme purifier (पवित्रम् उत्तमम्/holiest purification), directly perceivable, righteous, very easy to perform, and imperishable (अव्ययम्/eternal).
Men who have no faith (अश्रद्दधानाः/lack of trust) in this righteous path, O scorcher of foes (परन्तप/destroyer of enemies), fail to attain Me and return to the path of the mortal world (मृत्यु संसार वर्त्मनि/cycle of death and rebirth).
By Me all this universe is pervaded (ततम्/spread out) in My unmanifested form (अव्यक्त मूर्तिना/invisible shape); all living beings dwell in Me (मत्स्थानि/rest in Me), but I do not dwell in them.
And yet living beings do not dwell in Me; behold My divine mystery (योगम् ऐश्वरम्/lordly power of yoga); sustaining all creatures (भूतभृत्/supporter of beings) but not dwelling in them, My Self is the source of all beings (भूत भावनः/creator of living entities).
Just as the mighty wind moving everywhere always rests in space (आकाश स्थितः/abiding in ether), know that in the same manner all living beings rest in Me (मत्स्थानि/abiding in Me).
All living beings, O son of Kunti, go into My material nature (प्रकृतिम्/cosmic nature) at the end of a cosmic cycle (कल्प क्षये/destruction of the world); again, at the beginning of a cycle (कल्प आदौ/new creation), I send them forth.
Animating My own material nature (प्रकृतिम् स्वाम्/one's own cosmic energy), I send forth again and again this entire multitude of beings (भूत ग्रामम्/multitude of creatures), which is helpless under the control of nature (प्रकृतेः वशात्/under the power of material force).
And these actions do not bind Me (निबध्नन्ति/create bondage), O winner of wealth (धनञ्जय/Arjuna), sitting as one neutral (उदासीनवत्/unconcerned or detached) and unattached to those actions.
Under My supervision (मया अध्यक्षेण/by My presidency), material nature produces the moving and the non-moving world (सचराचरम्/animate and inanimate creation); for this reason, O son of Kunti, the universe revolves (विपरिवर्तते/cycles round).
Fools disregard Me (अवजानन्ति माम् मूढाः/ignorant people mock Me) when I assume a human form (मानुषीम् तनुम्/mortal body), not knowing My higher nature (परम् भावम्/supreme status) as the Great Lord of all beings.
Of vain hopes (मोघाशाः/empty desires), of vain actions, of vain knowledge, and senseless (विचेतसः/confused minds), they take shelter in the delusive nature (प्रकृतिम् मोहिनीम्/bewildering temperament) of fiends and demons.
But the great souls (महात्मानः/noble beings), O Partha, taking shelter in the divine nature (दैवीम् प्रकृतिम्/godly qualities), worship Me with undivided mind (अनन्या मनसः/single-pointed attention), knowing Me as the imperishable source of all beings.
Always chanting My praises (सततम् कीर्तयन्तः/constantly glorifying), striving with firm vows (दृढ व्रताः/resolute determination), and bowing down to Me in devotion, they worship Me, ever united with Me in meditation.
Others also, sacrificing with the wisdom-sacrifice (ज्ञान यज्ञेन/sacrifice of knowledge), worship Me as the One, as the Many, and as the manifold face of the universe (विश्वतोमुखम्/all-pervading form).
I am the Vedic ritual (क्रतुः/sacrifice), I am the sacrifice, I am the ancestral offering (स्वधा/oblation to ancestors), I am the healing herb (औषधम्/medicine), I am the sacred chant, I am the clarified butter (आज्यम्/ghee), I am the sacrificial fire, and I am the act of offering.
I am the father of this world, the mother, the sustainer (धाता/supporter), the grandfather, the object of knowledge (वेद्यम्/that which is to be known), the purifier, the syllable Om, and also the Rig, Sama, and Yajur Vedas.
I am the goal (गतिः/destination), the supporter, the lord, the witness (साक्षी/observer), the abode, the refuge (शरणम्/shelter), the dear friend, the origin, the dissolution, the foundation (स्थानम्/basis), the storehouse, and the imperishable seed (बीजम् अव्ययम्/eternal source).
I give heat (तपामि अहम्/I scorch), I withhold and send forth the rain (वर्षम्/rainfall); I am immortality (अमृतम्/deathlessness) and also death, and I am both existence and non-existence (सत् असत्/truth and untruth), O Arjuna.
The knowers of the three Vedas, the drinkers of Soma juice (सोमपाः/purified drinkers), cleansed of sins (पूत पापाः/sinless ones), worshipping Me with sacrifices, pray for the way to heaven; they reach the holy world of the lord of gods (सुरेन्द्र लोकम्/realm of Indra) and enjoy the divine pleasures of the gods in heaven.
They, having enjoyed that vast heaven-world (स्वर्ग लोकम् विशालम्/spacious celestial realm), enter the mortal world when their merit is exhausted (क्षीणे पुण्ये/depletion of pious deeds); thus conforming to the doctrine of the three Vedas and desiring desires (काम कामाः/lusting for pleasures), they obtain the state of going and returning.
To those persons who worship Me alone, thinking of no other (अनन्याः/without any other thought), who are ever-integrated (नित्य अभियुक्तनाम्/constantly connected), I bring full security and personal welfare (योग क्षेमम्/acquisition and preservation of goods).
Even those devotees who, endowed with faith (श्रद्धया अन्विताः/filled with devotion), worship other deities, they too worship Me alone, O son of Kunti, though contrary to the ancient rule (अविधि पूर्वकम्/informal or incorrect method).
For I alone am the enjoyer (भोक्ता/consumer) and also the Lord of all sacrifices; but they do not recognize Me in truth (तत्त्वेन/in reality), and therefore they fall from the spiritual path.
Those who vow to the gods go to the gods (देवान्/deities); those who vow to the ancestors go to the ancestors; those who worship the spirits go to the spirits; and those who worship Me come to Me (मद्याजिनः/My worshippers).
A leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water (तोयम्/liquid), whoever offers to Me with devotion, that offering of love from the pure-minded soul (प्रयत आत्मनः/self-controlled person) I accept.
Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in sacrifice (जुहोषि/pour as oblation), whatever you give away, and whatever penance you perform (तपस्यसि/undertake austerities), O son of Kunti, do that as an offering unto Me (मत् अर्पणम्/surrendered to Me).
Thus you shall be freed from the bonds of action (कर्म बन्धनैः/shackles of work) yielding good and evil fruits; with your mind intent on the yoga of renunciation (संन्यास योग/renunciation discipline), being liberated, you shall come to Me.
I am the same in all living beings (समः अहम्/equal I am); there is none hateful to Me nor dear; but those who worship Me with devotion (भक्त्या/reverence), they are in Me, and I am also in them.
Even if a person of highly wicked conduct (सुदुराचारः/very bad behavior) worships Me with undivided devotion, he must be regarded as righteous (साधुः/saintly), for he has rightly resolved (सम्याक व्यवसितः/properly determined).
Quickly he becomes a righteous soul (धर्मात्मा/virtuous person) and attains eternal peace (शान्तम्/tranquility); O son of Kunti, declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes (न प्रणश्यति/is never destroyed).
For, taking refuge in Me (व्यपाश्रित्य/sheltering), O Partha, even those who may be of sinful birth (पापयोनयः/lowly origin), women, traders, and laborers, they also attain the supreme destination (पराम् गतिम्/highest spiritual goal).
How much more then the holy priests (ब्राह्मणः पुण्याः/virtuous brahmins) and devoted royal sages (राजर्षयः/kings who are saints)! Having obtained this transient (अनित्यम्/temporary) and joyless world, worship Me.
Fix your mind on Me (मन्मनाः/absorbed in Me), become My devotee, sacrifice to Me, bow down to Me; having thus united your soul to Me and taking Me as your supreme goal (मत्परायणः/wholly dedicated to Me), you shall come to Me alone.




The Supreme Lord said: Listen again, O mighty-armed (महाबाहु/powerful warrior), to My supreme word (परम वचन/divine teaching), which I will speak to you for your benefit (हितकाम्यया/well-wishing), because you take pleasure (प्रीयमाणाय/beloved) in listening to Me.
Neither the hosts of gods (सुरगण/demigods) nor the great sages (महर्षि/great seers) know My origin (प्रभव/source of birth), because I am the source (आदि/beginning) of the gods and the great sages in every way (सर्वशः/completely).
He who knows Me as unborn (अज/birthless), without beginning (अनादि/sourceless), and the Supreme Lord of the universe (लोकमहेश्वर/master of all worlds), he, undeluded (असम्मूढ/free from confusion) among mortals (मर्त्येषु/human beings), is liberated from all sins (पाप/evil deeds).
Intelligence (बुद्धि/intellect), knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom), freedom from delusion (असम्मोह/clarity), forgiveness (क्षमा/patience), truth (सत्य/honesty), control of the senses (दम/self-restraint), control of the mind (शम/peacefulness), happiness (सुख/joy), distress (दुःख/sorrow), birth (भव/existence), death (अभाव/non-existence), fear (भय/terror), and fearlessness (अभय/courage) also,
Non-violence (अहिंसा/harmlessness), equality (समता/fairness), contentment (तुष्टि/satisfaction), austerity (तप/penance), charity (दान/giving), fame (यश/good reputation), and infamy (अयश/bad reputation)—all these different qualities (भाव/characteristics) of living beings (भूत/creatures) arise from Me alone.
The seven great sages (महर्षि/great seers) of old, and the four Manus (मनु/progenitors of mankind), were born from My mind, endowed with My nature (मद्भाव/divine essence), from whom all these creatures (प्रजा/population) in the world are descended.
He who truly knows this divine glory (विभूति/opulence) and mystic power (योग/spiritual energy) of Mine, becomes united with Me through unwavering (अविकम्प/shakeless) devotion (योग/connection); there is no doubt (संशय/suspicion) about this.
I am the source (प्रभव/origin) of everything; from Me everything originates (प्रवर्तते/emanates). Thinking thus, the wise (बुध/enlightened ones), filled with devotion (भावसमन्वित/loving feelings), worship Me.
With their minds fixed on Me (मच्चित्त/focused mind), with their lives surrendered to Me (मद्गतप्राण/devoted life), enlightening (बोधयन्त/teaching) one another and always talking about Me, they remain satisfied (तुष्ट/content) and rejoice (रमन्त/delighted).
To those who are constantly devoted (सततयुक्त/always connected) and worship Me with love (प्रीतिपूर्वकम्/affectionately), I give that yoga of intelligence (बुद्धियोग/divine understanding) by which they come to Me.
Out of pure compassion (अनुकम्पार्थम्/for showing mercy) for them, I, dwelling within their hearts (आत्मभावस्थ/situated in the soul), destroy the darkness born of ignorance (अज्ञानज तम/darkness of ignorance) with the shining lamp of knowledge (ज्ञानदीप/light of wisdom).
Arjuna said: You are the Supreme Brahman (परम ब्रह्म/ultimate reality), the supreme abode (परम धाम/highest sanctuary), the ultimate purifier (पवित्र/purest), the eternal (शाश्वत/timeless) divine (दिव्य/celestial) Person, the primeval God (आदिदेव/first deity), the unborn (अज/birthless), and the all-pervading Lord (विभु/omnipresent).
All the sages (ऋषि/seers) have declared this about You, as well as the divine sage (देवर्षि/celestial seer) Narada, Asita, Devala, and Vyasa; and now You Yourself are telling (ब्रवीषि/speaking) it to me.
I accept all this as truth (ऋतम्/correct fact) which You tell me, O Keshav. Indeed, O Lord (भगवन्/Supreme God), neither the gods (देव/demigods) nor the demons (दानव/demonic beings) understand Your manifestation (व्यक्ति/personality).
You Yourself alone know Yourself by Yourself, O Supreme Person (पुरुषोत्तम/best among men), O Creator of all beings (भूतभावन/source of creatures), O Lord of all beings (भूतेश/master of elements), O God of gods (देवदेव/lord of deities), O Master of the universe (जगत्पते/ruler of the world).
You should please tell me completely (अशेषेण/without leaving anything) about Your divine glories (आत्मविभूति/personal opulences), by which glories (विभूति/magnificent powers) You remain pervading (व्याप्य/occupying) all these worlds.
How may I know You, O Yogi (योगिन्/master of mysticism), by constant meditation (परिचिन्तयन्/deeply thinking) on You? In what various forms (भाव/aspects) or objects are You to be meditated upon (चिन्त्य/thought of) by me, O Lord (भगवन्/Supreme God)?
Describe to me again in detail (विस्तरेण/elaborately) Your mystic power (योग/divine energy) and glory (विभूति/opulence), O Janardana, because there is no satiety (तृप्ति/satisfaction) for me in hearing Your nectar-like words (अमृत/divine speech).
The Supreme Lord said: Yes, I will declare to you My divine glories (आत्मविभूति/personal opulences), but only the prominent ones (प्राधान्यतः/main ones), O best of the Kurus (कुरुश्रेष्ठ/Arjuna), for there is no end (अन्त/limit) to My detail (विस्तर/extension).
I am the Self (आत्मा/soul), O Gudakesha (गुडाकेश/conqueror of sleep), seated in the heart of all living beings (सर्वभूत/all creatures); I am the beginning (आदि/origin), the middle (मध्य/sustenance), and also the end (अन्त/destruction) of all beings.
Among the Adityas (आदित्य/solar deities) I am Vishnu; among luminous objects (ज्योतिषाम्/radiant bodies) I am the radiant sun (रवि/sun god); among the Maruts (मरुत्/wind gods) I am Marichi; and among the stars (नक्षत्र/constellations) I am the moon (शशी/lunar deity).
Among the Vedas (वेद/sacred scriptures) I am the Sama Veda; among the gods (देव/demigods) I am Vasava (इन्द्र/King Indra); among the senses (इन्द्रिय/sensory organs) I am the mind (मन/intellect); and in living beings I am consciousness (चेतना/life force).
Among the Rudras (रुद्र/destroyer deities) I am Shiva (शङ्कर/lord of auspiciousness); among the Yakshas and Rakshasas (यक्षरक्षसाम्/demigods and demons) I am the lord of wealth (वित्तेश/Kubera); among the Vasus (वसु/elemental gods) I am fire (पावक/Agni); and among the mountains (शिखरिणाम्/peaked mountains) I am Meru.
And know Me, O Partha (पार्थ/son of Pritha), as the chief among priests (पुरोधसाम्/spiritual guides), Brihaspati; among army commanders (सेनानीनाम्/generals) I am Skanda (कार्तिकेय/lord of war); and among water bodies (सरसाम्/lakes) I am the ocean (सागर/sea).
Among the great sages (महर्षि/great seers) I am Bhrigu; among words (गिराम्/speech) I am the single syllable Om (एकम् अक्षरम्/the one eternal sound); among sacrifices (यज्ञ/rituals) I am the chanting of holy names (जपयज्ञ/silent prayer); and among immovable things (स्थावराणाम्/fixed objects) I am the Himalayas.
Among all trees (सर्ववृक्ष/all plants) I am the Banyan tree (अश्वत्थ/holy fig tree); among divine sages (देवर्षि/celestial seers) I am Narada; among Gandharvas (गन्धर्व/heavenly musicians) I am Chitraratha; and among perfected beings (सिद्ध/realized souls) I am the sage Kapil (कपिल मुनि/the philosopher saint).
Among horses (अश्व/stallions), know Me as Uchchaishrava, born from the churning of nectar (अमृत उद्भव/produced from nectar); among lordly elephants (गजेन्द्र/king elephants) I am Airavata; and among men (नर/humans) I am the king (नराधिप/ruler of people).
Among weapons (आयुध/arms) I am the thunderbolt (वज्र/Indra's weapon); among cows (धेनूणाम्/cattle) I am Kamadhenu (कामधुक्/wish-fulfilling cow); among procreators (प्रजन/causes of generation) I am Kandarpa (कामदेव/god of love); and among serpents (सर्प/single-headed snakes) I am Vasuki.
Among many-headed Nagas (नाग/celestial serpents) I am Ananta (शेषनाग/the infinite snake); among water deities (यादसाम्/aquatic creatures) I am Varuna; among the ancestors (पितृ/departed forefathers) I am Aryama; and among controllers (संयमताम्/law enforcers) I am Yama (यमराज/god of justice).
Among demons (दैत्य/Asuras) I am Prahlada; among reckoners (कलयताम्/subduers) I am time (काल/duration); among beasts (मृग/wild animals) I am the king of beasts (मृगेन्द्र/lion); and among birds (पक्षिणाम्/winged creatures) I am Garuda (वैनतेय/son of Vinata).
Among purifiers (पवताम्/cleansing forces) I am the wind (पवन/air); among weapon-wielders (शस्त्रभृताम्/warriors) I am Rama; among fishes (झषाणाम्/aquatic animals) I am the shark (मकर/crocodile or sea monster); and among rivers (स्रोतसाम्/flowing streams) I am the Ganges (जाह्नवी/river Ganga).
Of creations (सर्ग/manifested worlds), I am the beginning (आदि/origin), the end (अन्त/conclusion), and also the middle (मध्य/existence), O Arjuna; among sciences (विद्या/knowledge) I am the spiritual science of the soul (अध्यात्मविद्या/sacred self-knowledge); and among debaters (प्रवदताम्/speakers) I am the logical conclusion (वाद/truthful discussion).
Among letters (अक्षर/alphabets) I am the letter 'A' (अकार/the first vowel); among compound words (सामासिक/grammar combinations) I am the dual compound (द्वन्द्व/equal combination); I alone am eternal time (अक्षय काल/imperishable duration), and I am the Creator (धाता/sustainer) whose face is everywhere (विश्वतोमुख/all-seeing).
I am all-devouring death (सर्वहर मृत्यु/life-taking end), and I am the source of birth (उद्भव/origin) for future beings (भविष्यताम्/things to come); among women (नारीणाम्/female qualities) I am fame (कीर्ति/good reputation), fortune (श्रीः/prosperity), fine speech (वाक्/eloquence), memory (स्मृति/remembrance), intelligence (मेधा/wisdom), firmness (धृति/determination), and forgiveness (क्षमा/patience).
Likewise, among Vedic hymns (साम्नाम्/songs of Sama Veda) I am the Brihat-sama; among poetic meters (छन्दसाम्/poetic verses) I am the Gayatri; among months (मासानाम्/calendar months) I am Margashirsha (नवंबर-दिसंबर का महीना/lunar month of Nov-Dec); and among seasons (ऋतूनाम्/weather cycles) I am spring (कुसुमाकर/the flower-bearing season).
I am the gambling (द्यूत/dicing game) of the cheats (छलयताम्/deceitful people); I am the splendor (तेज/brilliance) of the splendid (तेजस्विनाम्/glorious ones); I am victory (जय/triumph); I am effort (व्यवसाय/determination); and I am the goodness (सत्त्व/purity) of the virtuous (सत्त्ववताम्/pure-hearted people).
Among the Vrishni clan (वृष्णि/Yaduvansh) I am Vasudeva (कृष्ण/Krishna Himself); among the Pandavas (पाण्डव/sons of Pandu) I am Dhananjaya (अर्जुन/Arjuna); among the sages (मुनीणाम्/silent saints) I am Vyasa; and among thinkers (कवीनाम्/wise poets) I am the poet Ushana (शुक्राचार्य/teacher of Asuras).
Among punishers (दमयताम्/chastisers) I am the rod of punishment (दण्ड/justice rod); among those who seek victory (जिगीषताम्/desirous of winning) I am morality (नीति/righteous policy); among secrets (गुह्यानाम्/mysteries) I am silence (मौन/quietness); and I am the wisdom (ज्ञान/realization) of the wise (ज्ञानवताम्/knowledgeable persons).
And whatever is the seed (बीज/source of life) of all living beings (सर्वभूत/all creatures), that I am, O Arjuna; there is no being (भूत/entity), whether moving (चर/mobile) or non-moving (अचर/immobile), that can exist without Me (मया विना/separated from Me).
There is no end (अन्त/limit) to My divine glories (दिव्य विभूति/celestial opulences), O scorcher of enemies (परन्तप/Arjuna); this detail of My glory (विभूति विस्तर/extension of opulence) has been spoken by Me only as a brief description (उद्देशतः/as an example).
Whatever entity (सत्त्व/living being) is glorious (विभूतिमत्/powerful), prosperous (श्रीमद्/beautiful), or mighty (ऊर्जित/full of strength), know for certain (अवगच्छ/understand) that it originates from a mere fraction (अंशसम्भव/born of a part) of My splendor (तेज/divine energy).
But what is the need (किम्/what use) of this detailed knowledge (बहुना ज्ञातेन/knowing so much) to you, O Arjuna? I support (विष्टभ्य/sustaining) this entire universe (कृत्स्नम् जगत्/whole world) with a single fraction of My power (एकांशेन/with one small part), and so I exist.




Arjuna said: Out of compassion (अनुग्रह/mercy) for me, this most supreme mystery (परम गुह्य/highest secret) concerning the soul (अध्यात्म/spiritual knowledge) which You have spoken, by those words of Yours, this delusion (मोह/illusion) of mine is completely gone.
For the birth (भव/origin) and destruction (अप्यय/dissolution) of living beings have been heard by me in detail (विस्तरशः/elaborately) from You, O lotus-eyed Lord (कमलपत्राक्ष/one with eyes like lotus petals), and also Your imperishable greatness (माहात्म्य/majesty).
So it is, just as You have declared (आत्थ/spoken) Yourself to be, O Supreme Lord (परमेश्वर/highest master); yet I wish to see Your divine form (ऐश्वर रूप/majestic cosmic form), O Supreme Person (पुरुषोत्तम/best among men).
If You think (मन्यसे/consider) that it is possible (शक्य/achievable) for it to be seen by me, O Lord (प्रभो/master), O Lord of Yoga (योगेश्वर/master of mysticism), then please show me Your imperishable Self (अव्यय आत्मा/eternal divine form).
The Supreme Lord said: Behold, O Partha (पार्थ/son of Pritha), My hundreds and thousands of various forms (रूप/manifestations), which are divine (दिव्य/celestial) and of multicolored shapes and sizes (नानावर्णाकृति/different colors and structures).
Behold the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the Ashwinis, and also the Maruts; behold many wonders (आश्चर्य/miracles) never seen before (अदृष्टपूर्व/unseen earlier), O Bharata (भारत/descendant of Bharata).
Behold here, gathered in one place (एकस्थ/situated together), the entire universe (कृत्स्न जगत्/whole world) with all its moving and non-moving creatures (सचराचर/mobile and immobile), today inside My body, O Gudakesha (गुडाकेश/Arjuna), and whatever else you wish to see.
But you are not able (शक्य/capable) to see Me with these ordinary eyes of yours (स्वचक्षु/own physical eyes); I give you divine vision (दिव्य चक्षु/spiritual eyesight), so behold My majestic mystic power (ऐश्वर योग/divine opulence).
Sanjaya said: Having spoken thus, O King (राजन्/ruler), the great Lord of Yoga (महायोगेश्वर/master of mysticism), Hari (श्रीकृष्ण/Lord Krishna), then revealed to Partha His supreme majestic form (परम रूप/ultimate divine manifestation).
With many mouths and eyes (अनेक वक्त्र नयन/countless faces and vision), with many wonderful sights (अद्भुत दर्शन/miraculous appearances), adorned with many divine ornaments (दिव्य आभरण/celestial jewelry), and wielding many raised divine weapons (उद्यत आयुध/lifted arms).
Wearing divine garlands and garments (दिव्य माल्य अम्बर/celestial flowers and clothes), anointed with divine fragrances (दिव्य गन्ध अनुलेपन/sacred perfumes), full of all wonders (सर्व आश्चर्यमय/all-miraculous), the boundless God (अनन्त/infinite deity) facing all directions (विश्वतोमुख/omnipresent).
If the splendor of a thousand suns (सूर्य सहस्र/thousands of suns) were to blaze forth all at once (युगपत्/simultaneously) in the sky, that light (भाः/radiance) might resemble the brilliance of that great soul (महात्मा/supreme being).
There, gathered in one place in the body of the God of gods (देवदेव/lord of deities), the Pandava (पाण्डव/Arjuna) then saw the entire universe (कृत्स्न जगत्/whole world) divided into many parts (प्रविभक्तम् अनेकधा/separated in various ways).
Then he, Dhananjaya (धनञ्जय/Arjuna), filled with wonder (विस्मय आविष्ट/overwhelmed by amazement) and with his hair standing on end (हृष्टरोमा/thrilled with joy), bowed down his head to the Lord and spoke with folded hands (कृताञ्जलि/with joined palms).
Arjuna said: I see all the gods (देव/demigods) in Your divine body, O Lord, and also the assemblies of various classes of living beings (भूत सङ्घ/groups of creatures); Brahma, the master, seated on his lotus throne (कमलासनस्थ/sitting on lotus), and all the sages (ऋषि/seers) and divine serpents (उरग/celestial snakes).
I see You everywhere with countless arms, bellies, mouths, and eyes, possessing infinite forms (अनन्त रूप/boundless manifestations); I see neither Your end (अन्त/limit), nor Your middle (मध्य/sustenance), nor again Your origin (आदि/beginning), O Lord of the universe (विश्वेश्वर/master of cosmos), O Cosmic Form (विश्वरूप/universal manifestation).
I see You wearing a crown (किरीटिनम्/crowned lord), wielding a mace (गदिनम्/club-bearer) and holding a discus (चक्रिणम्/wheel-holder), a mass of splendor (तेजोराशि/heap of brilliance) shining everywhere, hard to look at (दुर्निरीक्ष्यम्/difficult to gaze), possessing the boundless radiance of blazing fire and the sun (दीप्त अनल अर्क द्युति/glowing fire and sun rays).
You are the imperishable (अक्षर/indestructible), the Supreme Being to be known (परमम् वेदितव्यम्/highest goal of knowledge), You are the ultimate resting place (परम् निधानम्/supreme sanctuary) of this universe; You are the changeless (अव्यय/imperishable) protector of eternal religion (शाश्वत धर्म गोप्ता/guardian of timeless righteousness), and You are the primeval Person (सनातन पुरुष/eternal divine being), this is my conviction.
I see You without beginning, middle, or end, possessing infinite power (अनन्त वीर्य/boundless strength), with countless arms, having the moon and sun as Your eyes (शशिसूर्य नेत्र/lunar and solar eyes), with Your mouth like a blazing sacrificial fire (दीप्त हुताश वक्त्र/burning fire face), scorching this universe with Your own radiance (स्वतेज/personal brilliance).
Indeed, this space between heaven and earth (द्यावापृथिव्योः अन्तरम्/gap between sky and earth) is completely filled by You alone, as well as all the directions (दिशः/quarters); seeing this wonderful (अद्भुत/miraculous) and fierce form (उग्र रूप/terrifying appearance) of Yours, the three worlds (लोकतय/three realms) are trembling with fear, O Great Soul (महात्मन्/exalted being).
Verily, these hosts of gods (सुरसङ्घ/demigod groups) are entering into You; some, terrified (भीत/frightened), are praising You with folded hands (प्राञ्जलि/joined palms); shouting "May there be peace!" (स्वस्ति/auspiciousness), assemblies of great sages and perfected souls (महर्षि सिद्ध सङ्घ/seers and realized saints) are singing hymns to You with excellent prayers.
The Rudras, the Adityas, the Vasus, and those known as the Sadhyas, the Vishvadevas, the twin Ashwinis, the Maruts, and the ancestors; the hosts of Gandharvas, Yakshas, Asuras, and Siddhas—all are looking at You in utter amazement (विस्मित/astonished).
Seeing Your massive form (महत् रूप/gigantic appearance) with many mouths and eyes, O mighty-armed warrior (महाबाहु/powerful armed one), with many arms, thighs, and feet, with many bellies, and terrifying with many tusks (दंष्ट्रा कराल/fearsome teeth), the worlds are trembling with fear (प्रव्यथित/distressed), and so am I.
Touching the sky (नभःस्पृशम्/reaching the heavens), glowing with many colors (अनेक वर्ण/countless hues), with wide-open mouths (व्यात्त आनन/gaping mouths), and with large blazing eyes (दीप्त विशाल नेत्र/glowing huge eyes); indeed, seeing You, my innermost soul is terrified (प्रव्यथित अन्तरात्मा/frightened heart), and I find neither courage (धृति/patience) nor peace (शम/tranquility), O Vishnu.
Seeing Your mouths terrifying with tusks (दंष्ट्रा कराल/fearsome teeth) and resembling the cosmic fire of destruction (काल अनल/fire of doom), I lose all sense of direction (दिशः न जाने/confused paths) and find no comfort (शर्म/happiness); have mercy (प्रसीद/be pleased), O Lord of gods (देवेश/master of deities), O Refuge of the universe (जगन्निवास/abode of worlds).
And all these sons of Dhritarashtra, together with the hosts of kings (अवनिपाल सङ्घ/groups of rulers), Bhishma, Drona, and that son of a charioteer, Karna (सूतपुत्र/Karna), along with the principal warriors on our side as well (योध मुख्य/chief soldiers),
Are rushing headlong into Your mouths, which are terrifying and dreadful with fearsome teeth (दंष्ट्रा कराल/frightful tusks); some are seen stuck between Your teeth (दशनान्तर/inside teeth gaps) with their heads crushed to pieces (चूर्णित उत्तमाङ्ग/smashed heads).
Just as the many rapid currents of rivers (नदीनाम् अम्बुवेगाः/water torrents of streams) flow directly towards the ocean (समुद्र/sea), so do these heroes of the human world (नरलोक वीर/warriors of mankind) enter Your blazing mouths (अभिविज्वलन्ति वक्त्राणि/glowing faces).
Just as moths (पतङ्ग/insects) rush with great speed into a blazing fire (प्रदीप्त ज्वलन/burning flame) only to meet their destruction (नाश/death), so do these people enter Your mouths with great speed only for their destruction.
You are licking Your lips (लेलिह्यसे/devouring continuously) while swallowing all these worlds from every side with Your flaming mouths (ज्वलद्भिः वदनैः/burning faces); filling the entire universe with Your radiance (तेज/brilliance), Your fierce rays (उग्राः भासः/terrible heat) are scorching it, O Vishnu.
Tell me who You are, possessing this fierce form (उग्र रूप/terrifying appearance); salutations unto You, O Chief of gods (देववर/supreme deity), please be gracious (प्रसीद/have mercy). I wish to know You, the primeval One (आद्यम्/original source), for I do not understand Your purpose (प्रवृत्ति/cosmic activity).
The Supreme Lord said: I am Time (काल/death), the destroyer of the worlds (लोक क्षय कृत्/world-annihilating force), now grown mighty and engaged here in wiping out the worlds. Even without your participation (ऋते अपि/excluding you), all these warriors arrayed in the opposing armies (प्रत्यनीक/enemy ranks) shall cease to exist.
Therefore, you stand up (उत्तिष्ठ/get ready) and gain glory (यश/good reputation); conquer the enemies (शत्रु/adversaries) and enjoy a prosperous kingdom (समृद्ध राज्यम्/flourishing empire). They have already been slain by Me alone beforehand; become a mere instrument (निमित्त मात्र/just a tool), O ambidextrous warrior (सव्यसाचिन्/Arjuna).
Slay Drona, Bhishma, Jayadratha, Karna, and also the other heroic warriors (योधवीर/brave soldiers) who are already killed by Me; do not be distressed (मा व्यथिष्ठाः/do not fear) or shake with pain. Just fight (युध्यस्व/wage war), and you will conquer your rivals (सपत्नान्/enemies) in battle.
Sanjaya said: Hearing these words (वचन/speech) of Keshava, the crowned one, Arjuna, with folded hands (कृताञ्जलि/joined palms) and trembling with fear (वेपमान/shivering), bowed down again and spoke to Krishna in a choked voice (सगद्गदम्/with an emotional voice), bowing low in extreme terror (भीतभीत/highly frightened).
Arjuna said: It is only proper (स्थाने/rightly so), O Lord of the senses (हृषीकेश/Krishna), that by Your praise (प्रकीर्त्या/glorification) the universe rejoices (प्रहृष्यति/feels delighted) and becomes attached to You; the demons, terrified (भीत/frightened), flee in all directions (दिशः द्रवन्ति/run away), and all the assemblies of perfected souls (सिद्ध सङ्घ/realized saints) bow down to You.
And why should they not bow down to You, O Great Soul (महात्मन्/exalted being), who are greater even than Brahma, being the original creator (आदिकर्ता/prime procreator)? O Infinite One, O Lord of gods (देवेश/master of deities), O Refuge of the universe (जगन्निवास/abode of worlds), You are the imperishable (अक्षर/indestructible), existence (सत्/manifest), non-existence (असत्/unmanifest), and that which is beyond both.
You are the primeval God (आदिदेव/first deity), the ancient Person (पुराण पुरुष/eternal being), You are the supreme resting place (परम् निधानम्/ultimate sanctuary) of this universe. You are the knower (वेत्ता/observer), the object of knowledge (वेद्यम्/that which is to be known), and the supreme abode (परम् धाम/highest goal); by You the entire universe is pervaded (ततम्/spread), O Lord of infinite forms (अनन्तरूप/boundless manifestations).
You are the wind god (वायु/air), the god of justice (यम/death), the fire god (अग्नि/blaze), the water god (वरुण/ocean deity), the moon (शशाङ्क/lunar deity), the creator Brahma (प्रजापति/lord of creatures), and the great-grandfather (प्रपितामह/ancestor of all). Salutations, salutations unto You a thousand times (सहस्रकृत्वः/thousands of times), and again and choices of times, salutations, salutations unto You.
Salutations to You from the front (पुरस्तात्/ahead), and also from behind (पृष्ठतः/rear); salutations be to You from all sides, O All-pervading soul (सर्व/everything). You possess infinite power (अनन्तवीर्य/boundless energy) and immeasurable strength (अमितविक्रम/limitless prowess); You pervade everything (समाप्नोषि/completely occupy), and therefore You are everything.
Thinking of You as a friend (सखा/companion), whatever I have rashly said (प्रसभम् उक्तम्/spoken rudely) like "O Krishna," "O Yadava," "O Friend," being ignorant of this majesty of Yours (महिमानम्/greatness), either due to carelessness (प्रमादात्/negligence) or out of affection (प्रणयेन/love) alone,
And for the sake of humor (अवहासार्थम्/jesting), in whatever ways You were dishonored (असत्कृतः/disrespected) while playing, resting, sitting, or eating together, whether alone or in the presence of others, O Infallible Lord (अच्युत/changeless God); for all that, I beg forgiveness (क्षामये/ask pardon) from You, the immeasurable One (अप्रमेयम्/unfathomable being).
You are the father of this world (लोकस्य पिता/parent of the universe), of all moving and non-moving beings; You are its worshipful object (पूज्य/venerable) and the greatest spiritual master (गुरु/teacher). There is no one equal to You (त्वत्समः/Your equal); how then can there be anyone greater than You in all the three worlds (लोकत्रय/three realms), O Lord of incomparable power (अप्रतिमप्रभाव/matchless glory)?
Therefore, bowing down (प्रणम्य/prostrating) and stretching my body on the ground (प्रणिधाय कायम्/offering respects with body), I seek to please You, the praiseworthy Lord (ईशम् ईड्यम्/adorable master). As a father forgives his son, a friend his friend, or a lover his beloved, You should please forgive me (सोढुम् अर्हसि/deserve to tolerate), O Divine Lord.
I am delighted (हृषित/overjoyed) having seen what was never seen before (अदृष्टपूर्व/unseen earlier), yet my mind is shaken with fear (भयेन प्रव्यथितम्/distressed with terror). Please show me that same divine form of Yours (देवरूप/gentle godly form); have mercy (प्रसीद/be gracious), O Lord of gods (देवेश/master of deities), O Refuge of the universe (जगन्निवास/abode of worlds).
I wish to see You just as before, wearing a crown (किरीटिनम्/crowned), holding a mace (गदिनम्/club-bearer) and a discus in Your hand (चक्रहस्त/wheel-holder). Please assume that same four-armed form (चतुर्भुज रूप/quadruple-armed manifestation), O thousand-armed Lord (सहस्रबाहु/infinite-armed one), O universal form (विश्वमूर्ते/cosmic personality).
The Supreme Lord said: Being pleased with you (प्रसन्नेन/gratified), O Arjuna, I have shown you this supreme universal form (परम् रूपम्/highest cosmic manifestation) through My own mystic power (आत्मयोगात्/divine energy). This form of Mine is full of splendor (तेजोमयम्/brilliant), universal, infinite, and primeval, which has never been seen before by anyone other than you.
Neither by the study of the Vedas and sacrifices (वेदयज्ञाध्ययन/scriptural rituals), nor by charity (दान/giving), nor by rituals (क्रिया/pious acts), nor by severe austerities (उग्र तप/harsh penances) can I be seen in this form in the world of men (नृलोक/human realm) by anyone else but you, O foremost hero of the Kurus (कुरुप्रवीर/Arjuna).
Let there be no fear (व्यथा/distress) and let there be no bewildered state of mind (विमूढभाव/confusion) in you upon seeing this terrible form (घोर रूप/fearsome appearance) of Mine. Free from fear (व्यपेतभीः/fearless) and with a happy heart (प्रीतमनाः/pleased mind), look again upon that very same form (रूप/manifestation) of Mine.
Sanjaya said: Having spoken thus to Arjuna, the son of Vasudeva, Krishna (वासुदेव/Lord Krishna) revealed His own four-armed form again; and the Great Soul (महात्मा/supreme being), assuming a gentle form once more (सौम्यवपुः/peaceful body), consoled the terrified Arjuna (भीत/frightened one).
Arjuna said: Seeing this gentle human form (मानुषम् सौम्यम् रूपम्/peaceful human appearance) of Yours, O Janardana, I have now become composed (सचेताः/tranquil) and have returned to my normal nature (प्रकृतिम् गतः/original state).
The Supreme Lord said: This form of Mine which you have seen is extremely difficult to behold (सुदुर्दर्शम्/rarely visible). Even the gods (देव/demigods) are constantly longing for a vision (दर्शनकाङ्क्षी/craving to see) of this cosmic form.
Neither by the Vedas, nor by penances (तप/austerity), nor by charity (दान/giving alms), nor by worship (इज्या/sacrificial rituals) can I be seen in this cosmic form as you have seen Me.
But by undivided devotion (अनन्या भक्ति/unswerving love) alone, O Arjuna, can I be known (ज्ञातुम्/understood), truly seen (द्रष्टुम्/perceived), and entered into (प्रवेष्टुम्/merged), O scorcher of enemies (परन्तप/Arjuna).
He who performs actions for Me (मत्कर्मकृत्/devoted worker), looks upon Me as the Supreme Goal (मत्परमः/regarding Me highest), is My devotee (मद्भक्त/loving worshiper), is free from attachment (सङ्गवर्जित/unattached), and bears no enmity towards any living being (निर्वैर/without hatred)—he comes to Me alone, O Pandava (पाण्डव/son of Pandu).




Arjuna said: Those who are thus always connected (हमेशा जुड़े हुए/always connected) devotees who worship you, and those who worship the indestructible (अविनाशी/indestructible) and unmanifested (अव्यक्त/unmanifested), who among them are the best knowers of yoga (योग के ज्ञाता/knowers of yoga)?
The Blessed Lord said: Those who fix their mind (मन लगाना/fix mind) on Me, who worship Me always united (नित्य युक्त/always united) with supreme faith (परम श्रद्धा/supreme faith), they are considered by Me as the most perfect in yoga (सर्वश्रेष्ठ योगी/most perfect in yoga).
But those who worship the indestructible, indefinable (अनिर्वचनीय/indefinable), unmanifested, all-pervading (सर्वव्यापी/all-pervading), inconceivable (अचिंत्य/inconceivable), changeless (कूटस्थ/changeless), immovable, and eternal (ध्रुव/eternal),
restraining all the senses (इन्द्रियों को वश में करना/restraining the senses), having equal mind (सम बुद्धि/equal mind) everywhere, they attain Me only, being engaged in the welfare of all beings (सबका भला/welfare of all beings).
Greater is the difficulty (कठिनाई/difficulty) for those whose minds are attached to the unmanifested; because the unmanifested goal (अव्यक्त गति/unmanifested goal) is attained with sorrow (दुःखपूर्वक/with sorrow) by the embodied ones (देहधारी/embodied ones).
But those who surrender all actions (कर्म सन्यास/surrender actions) to Me, regarding Me as supreme, and worship Me by meditating (ध्यान करना/meditating) with single-minded devotion (अनन्य भाव/single-minded devotion),
for them, whose minds are fixed on Me, O Partha, I become the savior (उद्धार करने वाला/savior) without delay from the ocean of the mortal world (मृत्यु रूपी संसार सागर/ocean of the mortal world).
Fix your mind on Me alone, establish your intellect (बुद्धि लगाना/establish intellect) in Me; thereafter you shall dwell in Me alone, there is no doubt (कोई संदेह नहीं/no doubt).
If you are not able to fix your mind (चित्त स्थिर करना/fix mind) steadily on Me, then seek to reach Me by the yoga of practice (अभ्यास योग/yoga of practice), O Dhananjaya.
If you are unable even to practice, become intent on My work (मेरे लिए कर्म/intent on My work); performing actions even for My sake, you shall achieve perfection (सिद्धि प्राप्त करना/achieve perfection).
If you are unable to do even this, taking refuge in My yoga (भगवान का आश्रय/refuge in My yoga), then renounce the fruits of all actions (कर्मफल का त्याग/renounce the fruits of actions) with a self-controlled mind (आत्मवान/self-controlled mind).
Better indeed is knowledge than practice, meditation (ध्यान/meditation) is superior to knowledge; from meditation comes the renunciation of the fruits of action (कर्मफल त्याग/renunciation of the fruits of action), and peace (शान्ति/peace) follows immediately after renunciation.
He who is free from malice (द्वेष न करने वाला/free from malice) towards all beings, friendly and compassionate (दयालु/compassionate), free from attachment (ममता रहित/free from attachment) and egoism (अहंकार रहित/free from egoism), balanced in pain and pleasure, and forgiving (क्षमाशील/forgiving),
always content (संतुष्ट/content), the yogi who is self-controlled (संयमी/self-controlled), of firm resolve (दृढ़ निश्चय/firm resolve), with mind and intellect dedicated to Me, that devotee of Mine is dear to Me.
He from whom the world is not agitated (परेशान नहीं होना/not agitated), and who is not agitated by the world, who is free from joy, envy (ईर्ष्या/envy), fear, and anxiety (चिंता/anxiety), he is dear to Me.
He who is free from expectations (इच्छा रहित/free from expectations), pure, expert (दक्ष/expert), indifferent (उदासीन/indifferent), free from pain, and free from all selfish undertakings (कर्मों का आरंभ त्यागने वाला/free from selfish undertakings), that devotee of Mine is dear to Me.
He who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves (शोक करना/grieves) nor desires, who renounces both good and evil (शुभ और अशुभ का त्यागी/renounces good and evil), and who is full of devotion (भक्तिमान/full of devotion), he is dear to Me.
He who is equal to enemy and friend, and also in honor and dishonor (मान और अपमान/honor and dishonor), equal in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, and free from attachment (आसक्ति रहित/free from attachment),

But those who worship this nectar of virtue (धर्म रूपी अमृत/nectar of virtue) as described above, full of faith (श्रद्धावान/full of faith), regarding Me as supreme, those devotees are exceedingly dear (अत्यंत प्रिय/exceedingly dear) to Me.




Arjuna said, I desire to know about nature (प्रकृति/matter), consciousness (पुरुष/spirit), the field (क्षेत्र/body), the knower of the field (क्षेत्रज्ञ/soul), knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom), and the object of knowledge (ज्ञेय/worth knowing), O Keshava.
The Blessed Lord said, This body, O son of Kunti, is called the field (क्षेत्र/physical body), and one who knows this is called the knower of the field (क्षेत्रज्ञ/conscious soul) by the experts (तद्विदः/scholars).
Know Me also as the knower of the field (क्षेत्रज्ञ/supreme soul) in all fields, O Bharata; the knowledge of the field and its knower is what I consider true knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom).
Hear from Me in brief (समासतः/short summary) what that field is, what its nature is, what its modifications (विकारी/changes) are, from where it arises, and who that knower is and what His powers (प्रभाव/glory) are.
This has been sung by sages (ऋषिभिः/seers) in many ways, in various distinct Vedic hymns (छन्दोभिः/verses), and also in the well-reasoned and conclusive (विनिश्चितैः/decisive) words of the Brahma Sutras.
The gross elements (महाभूत/five elements), the ego (अहङ्कार/sense of I-ness), the intellect (बुद्धि/intelligence), the unmanifest (अव्यक्त/unmanifested nature), the ten senses and the one mind, and the five sense objects (इन्द्रियगोचराः/objects of perception).
Desire, hatred, pleasure, pain, the physical body complex (सङ्घातः/combination), consciousness (चेतना/awareness), and fortitude (धृतिः/patience); this field has been briefly described along with its modifications (सविकारम्/with changes).
Humility (अमानित्वम्/absence of pride), pride-lessness (अदम्भित्वम्/honesty), non-violence (अहिंसा/not hurting others), tolerance (क्षान्तिः/forgiveness), simplicity (आर्जवम्/straightforwardness), service to the teacher (आचार्योपासनम्/respecting guru), purity, steadfastness (स्थैर्यम्/stability), and self-control (आत्मविनिग्रहः/mind control).
Detachment (वैराग्यम्/dispassion) toward sense objects, absence of ego (अनहङ्कारः/pridelessness), and deep reflection (अनुदर्शनम्/constant perception) on the faults and miseries (दोष/defects) of birth, death, old age, and disease.
Non-attachment (असक्तिः/dispassion), absence of intense attachment (अनभिष्वङ्गः/obsession) for son, wife, home, and others, and constant even-mindedness (समचित्तत्वम्/equanimity) in both desirable and undesirable events (उपपत्तिषु/occurrences).
Unswerving devotion (अव्यभिचारिणी भक्तिः/exclusive devotion) to Me through undivided yoga (अनन्य योगेन/focused connection), living in secluded places (विविक्त देश/lonely places), and distaste for the crowd of people (जन संसदि/social gatherings).
Constancy in spiritual knowledge (अध्यात्म ज्ञान/self-knowledge), and perception of the purpose of ultimate truth (तत्त्व ज्ञान/true wisdom); this is declared as knowledge, and whatever is opposite to this is ignorance (अज्ञानम्/darkness).
I shall now describe that which is to be known, by knowing which one attains immortality (अमृतम्/eternal life); that beginningless (अनादिमत्/without origin) supreme Brahman is said to be neither being (सत्/existence) nor non-being (असत्/non-existence).
With hands and feet everywhere, with eyes, heads, and faces everywhere, and with ears everywhere in the universe, that supreme reality exists, pervading everything (आवृत्य तिष्ठति/encompassing all).
Shining through the functions of all senses, yet devoid of all senses (सर्वेन्द्रिय विवर्जितम्/free from sense organs), unattached yet supporting everything (सर्वभृत्/sustainer), and free from qualities (निर्गुणम्/without attributes) yet the enjoyer of qualities.
Outside and inside all living beings, both moving (चरम्/animate) and non-moving (अचरम्/inanimate); because of its subtlety (सूक्ष्मत्वात्/fineness), it is incomprehensible (अविज्ञेयम्/unknowable), and it is far away as well as very near.
Undivided (अविभक्तम्/indivisible) yet remaining as if divided among all living beings; that object of knowledge is to be known as the sustainer of beings (भूतभर्तृ/guardian), the devourer (ग्रसिष्णु/destroyer), and the creator (प्रभविष्णु/generator).
That is said to be the light of even lights, beyond darkness (तमसः परम्/beyond ignorance); it is knowledge, the object of knowledge, and the goal of knowledge (ज्ञानगम्यम्/attainable by wisdom), seated in the hearts of everyone.
Thus the field, knowledge, and the object of knowledge have been briefly stated; My devotee, understanding this deeply (विज्ञाय/realizing), becomes fit for My nature (मद्भावाय/divine state).
Know that both nature (प्रकृतिम्/matter) and consciousness (पुरुषम्/spirit) are beginningless (अनादी/without origin), and know that modifications (विकारान्/changes) and qualities (गुणान्/modes of nature) are born of nature.
In the matter of creating the effect and the cause, nature (प्रकृतिः/matter) is said to be the cause (हेतुः/instrument), while consciousness (पुरुषः/soul) is said to be the cause in the experiencing (भोक्तृत्वे/enjoyment) of pleasures and pains.
For consciousness situated in nature experiences the qualities (गुणान्/modes) born of nature; attachment to these qualities (गुणसङ्गः/clinging to modes) is the cause of its births in good and evil wombs (योनि/species).
The supreme consciousness in this body is also called the onlooker (उपद्रष्टा/witness), the permitter (अनुमन्ता/sanctioner), the supporter (भर्ता/sustainer), the enjoyer (भोक्ता/experiencer), the Great Lord (महेश्वरः/supreme controller), and the Supreme Soul (परमात्मा/divine self).
He who thus knows the consciousness (पुरुषम्/spirit) and nature (प्रकृतिम्/matter) along with its qualities, even though living in any condition (सर्वथा वर्तमानः/acting in all ways), is not born again (भूयः अभिजायते/reborn).
Some perceive the Supreme Self within themselves through meditation (ध्यानेन/contemplation) by the purified mind, others by the yoga of knowledge (साङ्ख्येन योगेन/philosophy), and others by the yoga of action (कर्मयोगेन/selfless duty).
Yet others, not knowing this, worship by hearing from others (श्रुत्वा अन्येभ्यः/learning from teachers); they too cross over death, being fully devoted to what they hear (श्रुतिपरायणाः/trusting text).
Whatever being is born, whether non-moving (स्थावर/immobile) or moving (जङ्गमम्/mobile), know that it comes from the union (संयोगात्/combination) of the field and the knower of the field, O best of the Bharatas.
He who sees the Supreme Lord (परमेश्वरम्/god) existing equally in all living beings, the imperishable (अविनश्यन्तम्/immortal) within the perishing (विनश्यत्सु/mortal), he truly sees.
For seeing the same Lord equally established everywhere, he does not destroy himself by his own mind, and thereby he reaches the supreme goal (पराम् गतिम्/highest destination).
He who sees that all actions are performed in every way by nature (प्रकृत्या एव/by matter alone), and likewise sees the self as the non-doer (अकर्तारम्/not acting), he truly sees.
When he perceives the separated existence of living beings centered in One (एकस्थम्/resting in one), and expanding (विस्तारम्/evolution) from that alone, he then attains Brahman (ब्रह्म सम्पद्यते/realizes ultimate reality).
Being beginningless (अनादित्वात्/without origin) and free from attributes (निर्गुणत्वात्/without qualities), this imperishable (अव्ययः/changeless) Supreme Soul, O son of Kunti, though dwelling in the body, neither acts nor gets contaminated (न लिप्यते/is unaffected).
Just as the all-pervading space (आकाशम्/ether) is not contaminated because of its subtlety (सौक्ष्म्यात्/fineness), similarly the soul established everywhere in the body is not contaminated.
Just as the one sun illuminates (प्रकाशयति/lights up) this entire world, similarly the Lord of the field (क्षेत्री/soul) illuminates the entire field, O Bharata.
Those who thus perceive with the eye of knowledge (ज्ञानचक्षुषा/wisdom vision) the difference between the field and the knower of the field, and the liberation of beings from nature (भूतप्रकृतिमोक्षम्/freedom from matter), they attain the Supreme (परम्/highest goal).




The Blessed Lord said, I shall again declare to you the supreme wisdom (ज्ञानम्/knowledge), the best of all knowledge, knowing which all the sages (मुनयः/seers) attained the highest perfection (सिद्धिम्/salvation) from this world.
By taking refuge in this knowledge (ज्ञानम्/wisdom), they have attained unity (साधर्म्यम्/same nature) with Me; they are not born even during creation (सर्गे/manifestation), nor are they disturbed during dissolution (प्रलये/destruction).
My womb is the great nature (ब्रह्म/prakriti), in that I place the seed (गर्भम्/germ of life); from that, O Bharata, happens the birth (सम्भवः/origin) of all living beings.
In whatever wombs (सर्वयोनिषु/all species), O son of Kunti, various forms are produced, the great nature is their mother (योनिः/source), and I am the seed-giving father (पिता/creator).
Goodness (सत्त्वम्/purity), passion (रजः/activity), and ignorance (तमः/darkness) are the three qualities (गुणाः/modes) born of nature; they bind down, O mighty-armed one, the imperishable soul (देहिनम्/embodied self) in the body.
Of these, goodness (सत्त्वम्/purity), because of its stainlessness (निर्मलत्वात्/purity), is illuminating and healthy (अनामयम्/free from disease); it binds the soul through attachment to happiness (सुखसङ्गेन/desire for pleasure) and attachment to knowledge (ज्ञानसङ्गेन/intellectual pride), O sinless one.
Know passion (रजः/activity) to be of the nature of desire (रागात्मकम्/full of longing), born of craving and attachment (तृष्णा/greed); it binds down, O son of Kunti, the embodied soul through attachment to actions (कर्मसङ्गेन/work-driven mindset).
But know ignorance (तमः/darkness) to be born of darkness (अज्ञानजम्/born of delusion), which misleads (मोहनम्/deluding) all embodied beings; it binds them down, O Bharata, through negligence (प्रमाद/carelessness), laziness (आलस्य/sloth), and sleep.
Goodness attaches one to happiness (सुखे/pleasure), passion attaches one to action (कर्मणि/work), O Bharata; but ignorance, covering knowledge (ज्ञानम्/wisdom), attaches one to negligence (प्रमादे/carelessness).
Goodness arises, O Bharata, by overpowering passion (रजः/activity) and ignorance; similarly, passion arises by overpowering goodness and ignorance, and ignorance arises by overpowering goodness and passion.
When light (प्रकाशः/illumination), which is knowledge, shines through all the gates (सर्वद्वारेषु/sense organs) in this body, then one should know that goodness (सत्त्वम्/purity) has increased (विवृद्धम्/dominating).
Greed (लोभः/avarice), activity, undertaking of actions, restlessness (अशमः/lack of peace), and longing (स्पृहा/desire) arise, O best of Bharatas, when passion (रजसि/mode of activity) is increased.
Darkness (अप्रकाशः/lack of light), inactivity (अप्रवृत्तिः/laziness), negligence (प्रमादः/carelessness), and delusion (मोहः/confusion) arise, O joy of the Kurus, when ignorance (तमसि/mode of darkness) is increased.
When an embodied soul (देहभृत्/living being) meets death (प्रलयम्/dissolution) while goodness is predominant, then he attains the stainless worlds (लोकान्/realms) of the knowers of the highest truth (उत्तमविदाम्/wise scholars).
Meeting death in passion (रजसि/mode of activity), he is born among those attached to action (कर्मसङ्गेषु/work-oriented people); similarly, dying in ignorance (तमसि/mode of darkness), he is born in the wombs of the ignorant (मूढयोनिषु/foolish species).
The fruit of good action (सुकृतस्य/pious work) is said to be pure and stainless (सात्त्विकम्/good); but the fruit of passion is pain (दुःखम्/misery), and the fruit of ignorance is blindness (अज्ञानम्/foolishness).
From goodness arises knowledge (ज्ञानम्/wisdom), and from passion arises greed (लोभः/avarice); negligence (प्रमाद/carelessness) and delusion (मोह/misguidance) arise from ignorance, and also lack of wisdom (अज्ञानम्/darkness).
Those situated in goodness go upward (ऊर्ध्वम्/higher realms), those of passion stay in the middle (मध्ये/human world); those fixed in the lowest quality (जघन्यगुण/base nature) go downward (अधः/lower states), being full of ignorance.
When the seer (द्रष्टा/observer) perceives no doer other than the three qualities (गुणेभ्यः/modes of nature), and knows that which is higher (परम्/beyond) than the qualities, he attains My divine nature (मद्भावम्/spiritual state).
Crossing over these three qualities (गुणान्/modes) which are the source of the physical body (देहसमुद्भवान्/origin of body), the embodied soul is freed from birth, death, old age, and pain, and attains immortality (अमृतम्/eternal life).
Arjuna said, By what signs (लिङ्गैः/symptoms) is he known who has crossed over these three qualities, O Lord? What is his conduct (आचारः/behavior), and how does he go beyond (अतिवर्तते/transcend) these three qualities?
The Blessed Lord said, Illumination (प्रकाशम्/light), activity (प्रवृत्तिम्/action), and delusion (मोहम्/confusion), O son of Pandu; he does not hate them when they are present (सम्प्रवृत्तानि/active), nor does he long for them when they disappear (निवृत्तानि/ceased).
He who sits like one unconcerned (उदासीनवत्/neutral), and is not moved (विचाल्यते/shaken) by the qualities; who stays firm and does not waver (इङ्गते/shakes), knowing that only the qualities are acting (वर्तते/operating).
Equal in pain and pleasure, established in the self (स्वस्थः/self-poised), regarding a clod of earth, a stone, and gold as equal (सम/same value); wise (धीरः/patient), holding the pleasant and the unpleasant as equal, and equal in criticism (निन्दा/blame) and praise.
Equal in honor and dishonor (मान अपमानयोः/respect and insult), equal toward the sides of friends and enemies (मित्र अरि/ally and foe), and renouncing all selfish undertakings (सर्व आरम्भ परित्यागी/abandoning enterprises)—he is called beyond qualities (गुणातीतः/transcended).
And he who serves Me with unswerving (अव्यभिचारेण/unfailing) yoga of devotion (भक्तियोगेन/path of love), crossing over these three qualities, becomes fit (कल्पते/eligible) for becoming one with Brahman (ब्रह्मभूयाय/divine realization).
For I am the foundation (प्रतिष्ठा/abode) of Brahman, of the immortal (अमृतस्य/deathless) and the imperishable (अव्ययस्य/changeless), of eternal virtue (धर्मस्य/righteousness), and of absolute happiness (सुखस्य/ultimate bliss).




The Blessed Lord said, They speak of an imperishable (अव्ययम्/eternal) Banyan tree (अश्वत्थम्/holy fig tree) with roots above and branches below; its leaves are the Vedic hymns (छन्दांसि/verses), and he who knows this tree is a knower of the Vedas (वेदवित्/scholar).
Its branches spread below and above, nourished by the qualities (गुण/modes of nature), with sense objects (विषय/sensory pleasures) as shoots; and its roots extend downwards, binding one to actions (कर्म अनुबन्धीनि/entangled in deeds) in the world of humans.
Its real form is not perceived here in this world, neither its end (अन्तः/termination), nor its beginning (आदिः/origin), nor its foundation (सम्प्रतिष्ठा/basis); cutting down this firmly rooted Banyan tree with the strong weapon of detachment (असङ्ग शस्त्रेण/dispassion tool).
Then that goal (पदम्/destination) must be sought after, reaching which people do not return again (न निवर्तन्ति भूयः/never take rebirth); I surrender to that primeval consciousness (पुरुषम्/supreme spirit) from whom this ancient cosmic process (प्रवृत्तिः/manifestation) streamed forth.
Free from pride and delusion (निर्मान मोहाः/without vanity), conquering the evil of attachment (जित सङ्ग दोषाः/overcoming clinging), always devoted to spirituality (अध्यात्म नित्याः/self-focused), with desires fully quieted (विनिवृत्त कामाः/free from cravings), liberated from dualities (द्वन्द्वैः/opposites) known as pleasure and pain, the un-deluded reach that eternal goal (पदम् अव्ययम्/imperishable state).
Neither the sun illuminates (भासयते/lights up) that place, nor the moon (शशाङ्कः/lunar orb), nor fire (पावकः/flame); reaching where they never return, that is My supreme abode (धाम/dwelling).
An eternal fragment (सनातनः अंशः/immortal part) of My own self becomes the living soul (जीव भूतः/embodied spirit) in the world of life; it attracts the five senses and the mind as the sixth (मनः षष्ठानी/mind-inclusive), which are seated in nature.
When the master (ईश्वरः/soul) acquires a body (शरीरम्/physical frame) and when he leaves it, he takes these senses and goes, just as the wind carries scents (गन्धान्/fragrances) from their source (आशयात्/flowers).
Presiding over the ear (श्रोत्रम्/hearing organ), the eye (चक्षुः/sight), touch, taste, and smell, as well as the mind, this soul experiences the sense objects (विषयान/sensory inputs).
The deluded (विमूढाः/ignorant people) do not see the soul departing (उत्क्रामन्तम्/leaving), staying, or experiencing (भुञ्जानम्/enjoying) joined with the qualities; but those who possess the eye of knowledge (ज्ञान चक्षुषः/wisdom vision) truly see it.
The striving yogis (योगिनः/practitioners) see this soul established in themselves (आत्मनि अवस्थितम्/inside the self); but the undisciplined whose minds are impure (अकृतात्मानः/unrefined selves), even though striving, do not see this, being mindless (अचेतसः/devoid of wisdom).
That brilliance (तेजः/splendor) coming from the sun which illuminates the entire world, that which is in the moon and that which is in fire, know that brilliance to be Mine (मामकम्/belonging to Me).
Entering the earth (गाम्/soil), I support all living beings by My vital energy (ओजसा/strength); and I nourish all herbs and plants (ओषधीः/vegetation) by becoming the sap-giving moon (सोमः/lunar deity).
Becoming the digestive fire (वैश्वानरः/gastric fire) inside the bodies of living beings, and united with the incoming and outgoing breaths (प्राण अपान/breathing forces), I digest the four kinds of food (चतुर्विधम् अन्नम्/types of meals).
And I am seated in the hearts of everyone (हृदि सन्निविष्टः/inside the heart), from Me come memory (स्मृतिः/remembrance), knowledge, and their loss (अपोहनम्/forgetfulness); I am indeed the one to be known by all the Vedas, I am the author of the Vedanta (वेदान्तकृत्/upanishad creator), and the knower of the Vedas too.
There are two kinds of consciousness (पुरुषौ/beings) in this world, the perishable (क्षरः/mortal) and the imperishable (अक्षरः/immortal); all living beings are the perishable, and the unchanging soul (कूटस्थः/immobile self) is called the imperishable.
But the supreme consciousness (उत्तमः पुरुषः/highest spirit) is distinct, described as the Supreme Soul (परमात्मा/divine self); who, entering the three worlds (लोकत्रयम्/triple universe), sustains them as the eternal Lord (अव्ययः ईश्वरः/imperishable controller).
Since I am beyond the perishable (क्षरम्/mortal matter) and higher than even the imperishable, therefore I am celebrated in the world and in the Vedas as the Supreme Being (पुरुषोत्तमः/highest person).
He who, free from delusion (असम्मूढः/wise), thus knows Me as the Supreme Being, he knows everything (सर्ववित्/all-knowing) and worships Me with his whole being (सर्वभावेन/total devotion), O Bharata.
Thus this most secret scripture (गुह्यतमम् शास्त्रम्/hidden science) has been spoken by Me, O sinless one (अनघ/pure soul); understanding this, a person becomes wise (बुद्धिमान्/intelligent) and completes all his duties (कृतकृत्यः/fully accomplished), O Bharata.




The Blessed Lord said, Fearlessness (अभयम्/freedom from fear), purity of heart (सत्त्वसंशुद्धिः/inner cleansing), steadfastness in the yoga of knowledge, charity (दानम्/giving), control of the senses (दमः/self-restraint), sacrifice, study of sacred texts (स्वाध्यायः/self-study), austerity (तपः/penance), and straightforwardness (आर्जवम्/honesty).
Non-violence (अहिंसा/not hurting others), truthfulness (सत्यम्/honesty), freedom from anger (अक्रोधः/peacefulness), renunciation (त्यागः/detachment), tranquility (शान्तिः/peace), aversion to fault-finding (अपैशुनम्/not gossiping), compassion toward all living beings (भूतेषु दया/kindness to creatures), freedom from greed (अलोलुप्त्वम्/lack of avarice), gentleness (मार्दवम्/mildness), modesty (ह्रीः/shame), and absence of fickleness (अचापलम्/steadiness).
Vigor (तेजः/splendor), forgiveness (क्षमा/patience), fortitude (धृतिः/endurance), purity (शौचम्/cleanliness), freedom from malice (अद्रोहः/absence of hatred), and absence of pride (न अतिमानिता/humility); these qualities belong to those born with divine nature (दैवीम् सम्पदम्/godly virtues), O Bharata.
Hypocrisy (दम्भः/pretension), arrogance (दर्पः/pride), conceit (अभिमानः/vanity), anger (क्रोधः/fury), harshness (पारुष्यम्/cruelty), and ignorance (अज्ञानम्/lack of wisdom) belong to those born with demoniac nature (आसुरीम् सम्पदम्/evil traits), O Partha.
The divine nature is deemed for liberation (विमोक्षाय/salvation), and the demoniac nature for bondage (निबन्धाय/entanglement); do not grieve (मा शुचः/don't worry), O son of Pandu, you are born with divine virtues.
There are two types of created beings (भूतसर्गौ/classes of beings) in this world, the divine (दैवः/godly) and the demoniac (आसुरः/evil); the divine has been described at length (विस्तरशः/in detail), now hear from Me about the demoniac, O Partha.
Persons of demoniac nature do not know what to do (प्रवृत्तिम्/right action) and what to refrain from (निवृत्तिम्/wrong action); neither purity (शौचम्/cleanliness), nor good conduct (आचारः/behavior), nor truthfulness (सत्यम्/honesty) is found in them.
They say the universe is without truth (असत्यम्/false), without moral basis (अप्रतिष्ठम्/groundless), and without a God (अनीश्वरम्/rulerless); they claim it is brought about only by mutual union, driven by lust (कामहैतुकम्/caused by desire), and nothing else.
Holding fast to this view (दृष्टिम्/philosophy), these ruined souls (नष्टात्मानः/lost selves) of small intellect (अल्पबुद्धयः/low intelligence) rise as fierce workers of cruel deeds (उग्रकर्माणः/violent actions) for the destruction (क्षयाय/ruin) of the world, as its enemies.
Taking refuge in insatiable lust (दुश्पूरम् कामम्/unfillable desire), filled with hypocrisy, pride, and arrogance (दम्भमानमदान्विताः/full of vanity and conceit), holding false principles (असद्ग्राहान्/wrong notions) through delusion (मोहात्/ignorance), they act with impure resolves (अशुचिव्रताः/unclean vows).
Obsessed with countless anxieties (अपरिमेयाम् चिन्ताम्/endless worries) that end only with death (प्रलयन्ताम्/until dissolution), regarding the enjoyment of desires (कामोपभोग/sensory pleasure) as the highest goal, convinced that this is all.
Bound by hundreds of ties of desire (आशापाश/chains of hope), completely given over to lust and anger (कामक्रोधपरायणाः/driven by passion and fury), they strive to hoard wealth (अर्थसञ्चयान्/accumulation of money) by unjust means (अन्यायेन/illegal ways) for satisfying their desires.
This has been gained by me today, this desire (मनोरथम्/wish) I shall attain now; this wealth is mine, and this wealth (धनम्/money) will also be mine again in the future.
That enemy has been slain by me, and I shall slay other enemies as well; I am the lord (ईश्वरः/ruler), I am the enjoyer (भोगी/consumer), I am successful (सिद्धः/accomplished), powerful (बलवान्/strong), and happy (सुखी/joyful).
I am wealthy and of noble birth (आढ्यः अभिजनवान्/rich and aristocratic), who else is equal to me (मया सदृशः/like me); I will perform sacrifices, I will give charity, I will rejoice, thus they are deluded by ignorance (अज्ञान विमोहिताः/blinded by foolishness).
Bewildered by numerous thoughts (अनेक चित्त विभ्रान्ताः/confused by many anxieties), entangled in the net of delusion (मोह जाल/web of ignorance), and addicted to the enjoyment of desires, they fall into a foul hell (अशुचौ नरके/dirty purgatory).
Self-conceited (आत्मसम्भाविताः/pompous), stubborn (स्तब्धाः/obstinate), filled with the pride and madness of wealth (धन मान मद/riches and vanity), they perform nominal sacrifices (नामयज्ञैः/sacrifices in name only) out of ostentation (दम्भेन/for show), contrary to scriptural rules (अविधिपूर्वकम्/without rituals).
Taking refuge in egoism (अहङ्कारम्/pride), brute power (बलम्/strength), arrogance (दर्पम्/vanity), lust, and anger, these malicious people (अभ्यसूयकाः/envious critics) hate Me dwelling in their own and others' bodies.
These haters, cruel (क्रूरान्/merciless) and vicious, the lowest of men (नराधमान्/worst humans) in the world, I cast forever (अजस्रम्/continually) into miserable (अशुभान्/evil) and demoniac wombs (आसुरीषु योनिषु/degraded species).
Attaining the demoniac birth (आसुरीम् योनिम्/evil species), these deluded fools (मूढाः/ignorant souls) birth after birth, failing to reach Me, O son of Kunti, sink into the lowest state (अधमाम् गतिम्/worst destination).
Triple is this gate of hell (नरकस्य द्वारम्/entryway to damnation) that destroys the self (नाशनम् आत्मनः/ruin of soul)—lust (कामः/desire), anger (क्रोधः/fury), and greed (लोभः/avarice); therefore, one must renounce this trio (त्रयम् त्यजेत्/give up these three).
The man who is released from these three gates of darkness (तमोद्वारैः/pathways to ignorance), O son of Kunti, practices what is good for his soul (श्रेयः/welfare), and thereby reaches the supreme goal (पराम् गतिम्/highest destination).
He who casts aside the ordinances of scriptures (शास्त्रविधिम्/scriptural rules) and acts under the impulse of desire (कामकारतः/driven by whims), attains neither perfection (सिद्धिम्/success), nor happiness, nor the supreme goal.
Therefore, the scripture is your authority (प्रमाणम्/standard) in determining what ought to be done (कार्य/duty) and what ought not to be done (अकार्य/prohibited action); knowing what is declared in scriptural injunctions, you should perform action (कर्म कर्तुम्/do work) here in this world.




Arjuna said, What is the state (निष्ठा/standing) of those who set aside scriptural rules (शास्त्रविधिम्/ordinances) but perform sacrifices with full faith (श्रद्धया अन्विताः/devotedly), O Krishna; is it goodness (सत्त्वम्/purity), passion (रजः/activity), or ignorance (तमः/darkness)?
The Blessed Lord said, The faith (श्रद्धा/belief) of the embodied souls (देहिनाम्/living beings) is of three kinds, born of their own nature (स्वभावजा/inherent trait); it is goodness, passion, and ignorance, now hear about it (ताम् शृणु/listen to that).
The faith of everyone, O Bharata, is according to their inner nature (सत्त्वानुरूपा/mental disposition); a person is made of his faith (श्रद्धामयः/shaped by belief), and whatever his faith is, that he indeed is.
Those in goodness worship the gods (देवान्/deities), those in passion worship the demigods and demons (यक्षरक्षांसि/spirits and monsters), and other people who are in ignorance worship ghosts and spirits (भूतगणान्/phantom hosts).
Those senseless people (जनाः/persons) who perform terrible austerities (घोरम् तपः/severe penance) not prescribed by scriptures (अशास्त्रविहितम्/against rules), filled with hypocrisy and egoism (दम्भ अहङ्कार/vanity and pride), and driven by the force of desire and attachment (काम राग/lust and longing).
Torturing the group of elements (भूतग्रामम्/organs and tissues) in the physical body (शरीरस्थम्/dwelling in body), and also Me who resides within the inner body (अन्तःशरीरस्थम्/inside the heart); know them to be of demoniac resolves (आसुरनिश्चयान्/evil determinations).
Food also, which is dear (प्रियः/liked) to everyone, is of three kinds (त्रिविधः/threefold); and so are sacrifice (यज्ञः/worship), austerity, and charity; hear now this distinction (भेदम् इमम्/classification) among them.
Foods that increase life (आयुः/lifespan), purity of mind (सत्त्व/goodness), strength (बल/energy), health (आरोग्य/fitness), happiness (सुख/joy), and satisfaction (प्रीति/delight), and which are juicy (रस्याः/savory), wholesome (स्निग्धाः/nutritious), substantial, and pleasing to the heart, are dear to those in goodness (सात्त्विकप्रियाः/liked by pure minds).
Foods that are bitter (कटु/pungent), sour (अम्ल/acidic), salty (लवण/saline), excessively hot (अतिउष्ण/scorching), sharp (तीक्ष्ण/spicy), dry (रूक्ष/arid), and burning (विदाहिनः/inflammatory) are desired by those in passion (राजसस्य/active minds), producing pain, grief, and disease (आमय/sickness).
Food which is stale (यातयामम्/cooked hours ago), tasteless (गतरसम्/insipid), putrid (पूति/foul-smelling), rotten (पर्युषितम्/overnight leftovers), and also rejected scrap (उच्छिष्टम्/remnants of meals) and impure (अमेध्यम्/unfit for ritual), is dear to those in ignorance (तामसप्रियम्/liked by dark minds).
That sacrifice (यज्ञः/worship) which is offered according to scriptural rules (विधिदृष्टः/procedural guidelines) by those desiring no fruit (अफलाङ्क्षिभिः/without craving rewards), fixing the mind on the duty that it must be performed (यष्टव्यम् एव/sacrificing as duty), is of the nature of goodness.
But that which is offered seeking a reward (फलम्/result) and also for the sake of ostentation (दम्भार्थम्/for show), O best of Bharatas, know that sacrifice to be of the nature of passion (राजसम्/driven by vanity).
A sacrifice which is without scriptural rules (विधिहीनम्/disregarding procedures), in which no food is distributed (असृष्टान्नम्/without food charity), which is devoid of hymns (मन्त्रहीनम्/without chants), without priestly fees (अदक्षिणम्/without rewards), and devoid of faith (श्रद्धाविरहितम्/faithless), is declared to be of ignorance (तामसम्/dark nature).
Worship of the gods, the twice-born (द्विज/brahmin scholars), teachers (गुरु/spiritual masters), and the wise (प्राज्ञ/enlightened), purity (शौचम्/cleanliness), straightforwardness (आर्जवम्/honesty), celibacy, and non-violence are called the austerity of the body (शारीरम् तपः/physical discipline).
Speech which causes no agitation (अनुद्वेगकरम्/non-disturbing), which is truthful (सत्यम्/honest), pleasant (प्रिय/agreeable), and beneficial (हितम्/advantageous), and also the practice of studying scriptures (स्वाध्याय अभ्यसनम्/reciting texts) are called the austerity of speech (वाङ्मयम् तपः/verbal discipline).
Serenity of mind (मनःप्रसादः/mental peace), gentleness (सौम्यत्वम्/kindness), silence (मौनम्/reticence), self-control (आत्मविनिग्रहः/mind restriction), and purity of feelings (भावसंशुद्धिः/honesty of purpose); this is called the austerity of the mind (मानसम् तपः/mental discipline).
This threefold austerity (त्रिविधम् तपः/threefold penance) practiced with supreme faith (परया श्रद्धया/deep conviction) by disciplined men who desire no fruit (अफलाकाङ्क्षिभिः/without craving rewards) is declared to be of goodness (सात्त्विकम्/pure nature).
That austerity which is practiced for the sake of welcome (सत्कार/hospitality), honor (मान/respect), and worship (पूजार्थम्/adoration), and out of hypocrisy (दम्भेन/for show), is said here to be of passion (राजसम्/restless), unstable and temporary (अध्रुवम्/impermanent).
That austerity which is practiced through foolish delusion (मूढग्राहेण/stubborn ignorance) with self-torture (आत्मनः पीडया/pain to self), or for the purpose of destroying another (परस्य उत्सादनार्थम्/ruining enemies), is described as being of ignorance (तामसम्/dark nature).
That charity (दानम्/giving) which is given with the conviction that it ought to be given (दातव्यम् इति/duty to give), to one who makes no return (अनुपकारिणे/non-obliging person), at the right place (देशे/proper location), at the right time (काले/proper occasion), and to a worthy person (पात्रे/deserving recipient), is remembered as being of goodness.
But that which is given for the sake of a return benefit (प्रत्युपकारार्थम्/reciprocation), or looking for a fruit (फलम्/reward) later, or given with a grudging heart (परिक्लिष्टम्/reluctantly), that charity is remembered as being of passion (राजसम्/selfish nature).
That charity which is given at the wrong place and time (अदेशकाले/unsuitable location and hour), to unworthy persons (अपात्रेभ्यः/undeserving recipients), without respect (असत्कृतम्/insultingly) and with contempt (अवज्ञातम्/disdain), is described as being of ignorance.
"Om Tat Sat" (ॐ तत् सत्/divine syllables) is remembered as the threefold designation of Brahman (ब्रह्मणः निर्देशः/pointer to supreme reality); by that, the brahmins (ब्राह्मणाः/priestly scholars), the Vedas, and the sacrifices were ordained in the ancient past (पुरा/long ago).
Therefore, uttering "Om" (ॐ/sacred sound), the acts of sacrifice, charity, and austerity (यज्ञ दान तपः क्रियाः/pious rituals) as prescribed by scriptural rules always begin for the speakers of Brahman (ब्रह्मवादिनाम्/seekers of truth).
Uttering "Tat" (तत्/that supreme), without aiming at the fruit (फलम्/reward), various acts of sacrifice and austerity, and acts of charity (दान क्रियाः/giving deeds) are performed by those who desire liberation (मोक्षकाङ्क्षिभिः/seekers of salvation).
The word "Sat" (सत्/truth and goodness) is used in the sense of reality and goodness (सद्भावे साधुभावे/existence and righteousness); and also, O Partha, the word "Sat" is applied to a praiseworthy action (प्रशस्ते कर्मणि/auspicious deed).
Steadfastness (स्थितिः/stability) in sacrifice, austerity, and charity is also called "Sat"; and action for that sake (तदर्थीयम् कर्म/work for divine purpose) is also indeed designated as "Sat".
Whatever is offered in sacrifice, given in charity, practiced as austerity, or performed without faith (अश्रद्धया/faithlessly), is called "Asat" (असत्/untrue and worthless), O Partha; it is of no use here in this world nor hereafter (प्रेत्य/after death).




Arjuna said, O mighty-armed (महाबाहु/powerful arms), I want to know the true essence (तत्त्व/truth) of sannyasa (संन्यास/renunciation of desires) and of tyaga (त्याग/relinquishment), O Hrishikesha, individually (पृथक्/separately), O killer of Keshi (केशिनिषूदन/Krishna).
The Supreme Lord said, The wise people know the renunciation (न्यास/giving up) of desire-driven actions (काम्य कर्म/motivated actions) as sannyasa (संन्यास/renunciation), and the learned (विचक्षण/experts) declare the abandonment (त्याग/giving up) of the fruits of all actions (कर्मफल/results of deeds) as tyaga (त्याग/relinquishment).
Some scholars (मनीषी/wise thinkers) say that action should be abandoned (त्याज्यम्/given up) like an evil (दोषवत्/faulty), and others say that the actions of sacrifice (यज्ञ/yajna), charity (दान/charity) and penance (तपः/austerity) should not be abandoned (न त्याज्यम्/must not be given up).
Hear My final conclusion (निश्चय/decision) about that relinquishment (त्याग/abandonment), O best of the Bharatas (भरतसत्तम/Arjuna), for relinquishment (त्याग/giving up), O tiger among men (पुरुषव्याघ्र/heroic man), is declared to be of three types (त्रिविधः/three kinds).
Action involving sacrifice (यज्ञ/yajna), charity (दान/charity) and penance (तपः/austerity) should not be abandoned (न त्याज्यम्/must not be given up), indeed it must be done (कार्यम्/duty), because sacrifice (यज्ञ/yajna), charity (दान/charity) and penance (तपः/austerity) are purifiers (पावन/cleansing agents) of the wise scholars (मनीषी/wise thinkers).
But even these actions (कर्म/deeds) must be performed, O Partha, by abandoning attachment (सङ्ग/attachment) and the fruits (फल/results) also, this is My certain (निश्चित/firm) and best opinion (मत/belief).
But the renunciation (संन्यास/giving up) of obligatory duty (नियत कर्म/prescribed duty) is not proper (न उपपद्यते/not right), its abandonment (परित्याग/giving up) out of delusion (मोह/ignorance) is declared to be tamasic (तामस/dark or ignorant character).
Whatever action (कर्म/deed) one abandons (त्यजेत्/gives up) out of fear of bodily pain (कायक्लेश/physical suffering), thinking it is troublesome (दुःखम्/painful), he, performing that rajasic abandonment (राजस त्याग/passionate giving up), will certainly not gain the fruit of relinquishment (त्यागफल/benefit of renunciation).
Whatever obligatory duty (नियत कर्म/prescribed action) is performed, O Arjuna, just because it ought to be done (कार्यम्/duty), by abandoning attachment (सङ्ग/attachment) and the fruit (फल/result) also, that relinquishment (त्याग/giving up) is considered sattvic (सात्त्विक/pure or righteous).
He does not hate a disagreeable action (अकुशल कर्म/unpleasant task) nor does he get attached (अनुषज्जते/cling) to an agreeable action (कुशल कर्म/pleasant task), that relinquisher (त्यागी/renouncer) who is filled with purity (सत्त्वसमाविष्ट/imbued with goodness), intelligent (मेधावी/wise), and free from doubts (छिन्नसंशय/doubts cleared).
It is indeed not possible (न शक्यम्/not possible) for a human being (देहभृत्/embodied soul) to abandon actions (कर्म/deeds) completely (अशेषतः/entirely), but he who abandons the fruits of actions (कर्मफलत्यागी/giver of action results), he is called (अभिधीयते/named) a true relinquisher (त्यागी/renouncer).
The three-fold (त्रिविधम्/three kinds) fruit of action (कर्मफल/results of deeds) namely undesirable (अनिष्ट/bad), desirable (इष्ट/good) and mixed (मिश्र/mixture), comes to non-renouncers (अत्यागी/those who do not give up) after death (प्रेत्य/after leaving body), but never to sannyasis (संन्यासी/monks or true renouncers) anywhere.
Learn from Me, O mighty-armed (महाबाहु/powerful arms), these five (पञ्च/five) causes (कारण/reasons) which are declared in the Sankhya philosophy (साङ्ख्य कृतान्त/philosophical conclusion) for the fulfillment (सिद्धि/accomplishment) of all actions (सर्वकर्म/all deeds).
The place of action (अधिष्ठान/body or base), also the doer (कर्ता/agent), the various instruments (करण/senses), the distinct varied efforts (चेष्टा/activities), and divine providence (दैव/destiny) as the fifth (पञ्चम/fifth) among these.
Whatever action (कर्म/deed) a man starts (प्रारभते/begins) with his body (शरीर/physical form), speech (वाक्/words) or mind (मनस्/thoughts), whether right (न्याय्य/lawful) or reverse (विपरीत/unjust), these five are its causes (हेतु/reasons).
That being the case (तत्र एवम् सति/this being so), he who sees only the pure self (केवल आत्मा/soul alone) as the doer (कर्ता/agent) due to an untrained intellect (अकृतबुद्धित्व/unrefined intellect), that foolish-minded person (दुर्मति/perverted mind) does not see the truth.
He whose state of mind is free from egoism (अहङ्कृत भाव/sense of I-ness) and whose intellect (बुद्धि/understanding) is not stained (लिप्यते/tainted), even if he kills these worlds (लोक/people), he does not kill, nor is he bound (निबध्यते/tied by karma).
Knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom), the object of knowledge (ज्ञेय/to be known), and the knower (परिज्ञाता/the understander) are the three-fold impulse to action (कर्मचोदना/instigation to act), the instrument (करण/senses), the action (कर्म/deed) and the doer (कर्ता/agent) are the three-fold components of action (कर्मसङ्ग्रह/accumulation of action).
Knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom), action (कर्म/deed) and the doer (कर्ता/agent) are declared to be of three types (त्रिधा/three kinds) only according to the distinction of components (गुणभेद/division of qualities) in the science of qualities (गुणसङ्ख्यान/classification of attributes), hear about them also properly (यथावत्/as they are).
That knowledge by which one sees one imperishable existence (अव्यय भाव/eternal reality) in all living beings (सर्वभूत/all creatures), undivided (अविभक्त/one whole) in the divided ones (विभक्त/separated things), know that knowledge to be sattvic (सात्त्विक/pure).
But that knowledge which sees many distinct existences (नानाभाव/different natures) of various kinds (पृथग्विध/diverse types) in all living beings (सर्वभूत/all creatures) due to separateness (पृथक्त्व/individuality), know that knowledge to be rajasic (राजस/passionate).
But that knowledge which is attached (सक्त/clung) to one single effect or task (कार्य/action) as if it were the whole (कृत्स्नवत्/complete reality), which is without reason (अहेतुक/baseless), without true essence (अतत्त्वार्थवत्/lacking truth) and narrow (अल्प/petty), that is declared to be tamasic (तामस/dark or ignorant).
An obligatory action (नियत कर्म/prescribed duty) which is free from attachment (सङ्गरहित/without cling), done without liking or disliking (अरागद्वेषतः/without love and hate), by one who does not desire the fruit (अफलप्रेप्सु/not seeking results), that is called sattvic (सात्त्विक/pure).
But that action which is performed with great effort (बहुलायाश/heavy strain) by one who desires gratification of desires (कामेप्सु/seeking enjoyments) or again with egoism (साहङ्कार/proudly), that is declared to be rajasic (राजस/passionate).
That action which is started out of delusion (मोह/blind ignorance), without considering the future consequences (अनुबन्ध/result), loss (क्षय/damage), injury to others (हिंसा/harm) and one's own capability (पौरुष/capacity), that is called tamasic (तामस/dark or ignorant).
A doer (कर्ता/agent) who is free from attachment (मुक्तसङ्ग/detached), free from egoistic speech (अनहंवादी/not bragging), endowed with patience (धृति/fortitude) and enthusiasm (उत्साह/zeal), and unchanged (निर्विकार/unmoved) by success or failure (सिद्धिअसिद्धि/gain and loss), is called sattvic (सात्त्विक/pure).
A doer (कर्ता/agent) who is passionate (रागी/full of attachment), desirous of the fruits of action (कर्मफलप्रेप्सु/seeking results), greedy (लुब्ध/covetous), cruel-natured (हिंसात्मक/harmful), impure (अशुचि/unclean), and filled with joy and sorrow (हर्षशोक अन्वित/moved by pleasure and grief), is declared to be rajasic (राजस/passionate).
A doer (कर्ता/agent) who is undisciplined (अयुक्त/unsteady), vulgar (प्राकृत/unrefined), stubborn (स्तब्ध/obstinate), deceitful (शठ/dishonest), malicious (नैष्कृतिक/insulting), lazy (अलस/slothful), depressed (विषादी/gloomy) and a procrastinator (दीर्घसूत्री/delaying things), is called tamasic (तामस/dark or ignorant).
Hear the three-fold (त्रिविधम्/three kinds) division of intellect (बुद्धि/understanding) and also of patience (धृति/fortitude) according to the qualities (गुण/attributes), as I tell you completely (अशेषेण/fully) and individually (पृथक्त्वेन/separately), O Dhananjaya (धनञ्जय/Arjuna).
That intellect which knows right action (प्रवृत्ति/active involvement) and cessation of action (निवृत्ति/renunciation), what ought to be done (कार्य/duty) and what ought not to be done (अकार्य/wrong deed), fear (भय/terror) and fearlessness (अभय/courage), bondage (बन्ध/captivity) and liberation (मोक्ष/freedom), that, O Partha, is sattvic intellect (सात्त्विकी बुद्धि/pure understanding).
That intellect by which one mistakenly understands (अयथावत् प्रजानाति/knows incorrectly) righteousness (धर्म/duty) and unrighteousness (अधर्म/sin), and also what ought to be done (कार्य/duty) and what ought not to be done (अकार्य/wrong deed), that, O Partha, is rajasic intellect (राजसी बुद्धि/passionate understanding).
That intellect which, enveloped in darkness (तमसा आवृत/covered in ignorance), thinks unrighteousness (अधर्म/sin) to be righteousness (धर्म/duty) and sees all things in a distorted way (विपरीत/perverted), that, O Partha, is tamasic intellect (तामसी बुद्धि/ignorant understanding).
The unswerving patience (अव्यभिचारिणी धृति/constant fortitude) by which one sustains the activities of the mind (मनस्/thoughts), life-breaths (प्राण/vital energies) and senses (इन्द्रिय/sensory organs) through yoga (योग/meditation), that patience, O Partha, is sattvic (सात्त्विकी/pure).
But that patience, O Arjuna, by which a person holding onto righteousness (धर्म/duty), desire (काम/pleasure) and wealth (अर्थ/money) out of intense attachment (प्रसङ्ग/clinging), desires the fruits (फलाकाङ्क्षी/longing for results), that patience, O Partha, is rajasic (राजसी/passionate).
That patience by which a foolish-minded person (दुर्मेधा/stupid intellect) does not give up sleep (स्वप्न/slumber), fear (भय/terror), grief (शोक/sorrow), depression (विषाद/gloominess) and also arrogance (मद/pride), that patience, O Partha, is tamasic (तामसी/ignorant).
Now hear from Me, O best of the Bharatas (भरतर्षभ/Arjuna), about the three kinds (त्रिविधम्/three-fold) of happiness (सुख/pleasure), in which one rejoices through practice (अभ्यास/repetition) and surely reaches the end of pain (दुःखान्त/termination of sorrow).
That happiness which is like poison (विष/toxin) in the beginning (अग्र/start) but like nectar (अमृत/elixir) in the end result (परिणाम/consequence), that happiness is declared to be sattvic (सात्त्विक/pure), born from the clarity (प्रसाद/purity) of one's own intellect (आत्मबुद्धि/self-understanding).
That happiness which arises from the contact of senses with objects (विषय इन्द्रिय संयोग/sensory-object contact) and is like nectar (अमृत/elixir) in the beginning (अग्र/start) but like poison (विष/toxin) in the end result (परिणाम/consequence), that happiness is remembered as rajasic (राजस/passionate).
That happiness which both in the beginning (अग्र/start) and in the future consequence (अनुबन्ध/sequel) deludes the self (मोहनम् आत्मनः/blinding the soul), and which arises from sleep (निद्रा/slumber), laziness (आलस्य/sloth) and negligence (प्रमाद/carelessness), that is declared to be tamasic (तामस/dark or ignorant).
There is not that entity (सत्त्व/being) on earth (पृथिवी/world) or again in heaven (दिवि/sky) among the gods (देव/celestials), which can be free (मुक्त/liberated) from these three qualities born of nature (प्रकृतिज गुण/material attributes).
The duties (कर्म/actions) of the Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras, O scorcher of enemies (परन्तप/Arjuna), are distributed (प्रविभक्त/divided) according to the qualities born of their own nature (स्वभाव प्रभव गुण/innate characteristics).
Control of the mind (शम/peacefulness), control of the senses (दम/self-restraint), austerity (तपः/penance), purity (शौचम्/cleanliness), forgiveness (क्षान्तिः/tolerance), straightforwardness (आर्जवम्/honesty) indeed, and also knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom), experimental knowledge (विज्ञान/realization) and faith in God (आस्तिक्यम्/belief), are the natural duties of a Brahmin (ब्रह्मकर्म स्वभावजम्/inherent priestly tasks).
Heroism (शौर्यम्/bravery), majesty (तेजः/splendor), fortitude (धृतिः/patience), dexterity (दाक्ष्यम्/skill), and also not fleeing from battle (युद्धे अपलायनम्/not retreating from war), charity (दानम्/generosity) and leadership quality (ईश्वरभाव/lordliness), are the natural duties of a Kshatriya (क्षात्रकर्म स्वभावजम्/inherent warrior tasks).
Agriculture (कृषि/farming), cow protection (गोरक्ष्य/cattle rearing) and trade (वाणिज्यम्/business) are the natural duties of a Vaishya (वैश्यकर्म स्वभावजम्/inherent mercantile tasks), and action consisting of service (परिचर्यात्मक कर्म/service-oriented work) is also the natural duty of a Shudra (शूद्रकर्म स्वभावजम्/inherent labor tasks).
Devoted to his own respective duty (स्वे स्वे कर्मणि अभिरतः/engrossed in own work), a man attains perfection (संसिद्धि/highest success); hear how one who is devoted to his own duty (स्वकर्म निरतः/absorbed in own task) finds perfection (सिद्धि/accomplishment).
From whom comes the origin (प्रवृत्ति/creation) of all living beings (भूत/creatures) and by whom all this universe is pervaded (तत/spread), by worshipping Him (अभ्यर्च्य/venerating) through one's own duty (स्वकर्मणा/by own action), a human being finds perfection (सिद्धि/success).
Better is one's own duty (स्वधर्म/own duty) though deficient in qualities (विगुण/imperfect) than the duty of another (परधर्म/another's duty) well-performed (स्वनुष्ठित/done perfectly); performing action prescribed by one's own nature (स्वभाव नियत कर्म/nature-ordained task), one does not incur sin (किल्बिषम्/fault).
One should not abandon (न त्यजेत्/must not give up) innate duty (सहज कर्म/born-with action), O son of Kunti (कौन्तेय/Arjuna), even though it is full of faults (सदोष/faulty), for all undertakings (आरम्भ/ventures) are covered by defects (दोष/faults) just as fire is covered by smoke (धूम/smoke).
He whose intellect is unattached (असक्त बुद्धि/detached mind) everywhere, who has conquered his self (जित आत्मा/controlled mind) and is free from desires (विगत स्पृह/without cravings), attains the supreme perfection of actionlessness (परमा नैष्कर्म्य सिद्धि/highest state beyond karma) through renunciation (संन्यास/sannyasa).
Learn from Me in brief (समासेन/shortly), O son of Kunti (कौन्तेय/Arjuna), how one who has attained perfection (सिद्धि/success) also attains the ultimate reality (ब्रह्म/Brahman), which is the supreme consummation (परा निष्ठा/highest culmination) of knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom).
Endowed with a pure intellect (विशुद्ध बुद्धि/cleansed understanding), regulating oneself (आत्मानम् नियम्य/controlling the self) with firmness (धृति/fortitude), abandoning sound and other sense objects (शब्दादि विषय/sensory inputs), and casting aside attachment and hatred (रागद्वेष/liking and disliking).
Living in a lonely place (विविक्त सेवी/residing in solitude), eating lightly (लघ्वाशी/moderate diet), controlling speech, body and mind (यत वाक् काय मानस/restrained words, physique and thoughts), constantly absorbed in meditation (ध्यान योग पर/dedicated to dhyana), and taking firm refuge in dispassion (वैराग्यम् समुपाश्रित/completely sheltered in detachment).
Having abandoned egoism (अहङ्कार/pride), brute force (बल/power), arrogance (दर्पम्/vanity), desire (कामम्/lust), anger (क्रोधम्/wrath) and possessiveness (परिग्रहम्/hoarding), free from the notion of 'mine' (निर्मम/without ownership) and peaceful (शान्त/tranquil), he is fit for becoming one with Brahman (ब्रह्मभूयाय कल्पते/eligible for divine union).
Becoming one with Brahman (ब्रह्मभूत/attaining divine state) and having a cheerful soul (प्रसन्न आत्मा/joyful mind), he neither grieves (न शोचति/does not lament) nor desires (न काङ्क्षति/does not crave); being same to all living beings (समः सर्वेषु भूतेषु/equal to all creatures), he attains supreme devotion to Me (परा मद्भक्ति/highest devotion to Me).
Through devotion (भक्त्या/by love) he knows Me truly (तत्त्वतः/in reality), how much and who I am; then, having known Me in truth (तत्त्वतः ज्ञात्वा/knowing reality), he enters into Me (विशते/merges) immediately after that (तद् अनन्तरम्/following that).
Though performing all actions (सर्व कर्माणि/all deeds) at all times, one who takes exclusive refuge in Me (मद्व्यपाश्रय/sheltered in Me) attains the eternal (शाश्वत/permanent) and imperishable state (अव्यय पद/undecaying goal) by My grace (मत्प्रसाद/My blessing).
Mentally surrendering all actions (सर्व कर्माणि संन्यस्य/dedicating all deeds) to Me, regarding Me as the supreme goal (मत्पर/devoted to Me), and taking refuge in the yoga of intellect (बुद्धियोग/yoga of understanding), fix your mind on Me constantly (सततम् मच्चित्तः भव/always keep consciousness in Me).
Fixing your mind on Me (मच्चित्तः/mind in Me), you shall cross over (तरिष्यसि/will overcome) all difficulties (सर्व दुर्गाणि/all hurdles) by My grace (मत्प्रसाद/My blessing); but if out of egoism (अहङ्कार/vanity) you will not listen (न श्रोष्यसि/will not hear), you will perish (विनङ्क्ष्यसि/will be destroyed).
If, taking refuge in egoism (अहङ्कार/vanity), you think "I will not fight" (न योत्स्ये/will not wage war), this resolve of yours is vain (मिथ्या व्यवसाय/false decision), for your own material nature (प्रकृति/innate disposition) will compel you (नियोक्ष्यति/will force you).
Bound (निबद्ध/chained) by your own duty born of nature (स्वभावज कर्म/innate action), O son of Kunti (कौन्तेय/Arjuna), that which you do not wish to do (कर्तुम् न इच्छसि/do not want to perform) out of delusion (मोह/ignorance), you will do that even helplessly (अवशः अपि/forced by nature).
The Supreme Lord (ईश्वरः/God) dwells in the heart region (हृद्देश/heart center) of all living beings (सर्वभूत/all creatures), O Arjuna, causing all living beings to spin (भ्रामयन्/revolving) by His deceptive power (माया/illusion) as if they were mounted on a machine (यन्त्रारूढ/seated on a carousel).
Take refuge in Him alone (तम् एव शरणम् गच्छ/surrender to Him only) with your entire being (सर्वभावेन/with whole heart), O Bharata (भारत/Arjuna); by His grace (तत्प्रसाद/His blessing) you shall attain supreme peace (परा शान्तम्/ultimate tranquility) and the eternal abode (शाश्वत स्थान/permanent residence).
Thus has this knowledge (ज्ञान/wisdom), which is more secret than secrecy itself (गुह्यात् गुह्यतरम्/more mysterious than hidden), been declared to you by Me; reflecting on this fully (विमृश्य एतत् अशेषेण/deliberating on this completely), do as you wish (यथा इच्छसि तथा कुरु/act as you like).
Hear again My supreme word (परमम् वचः/highest teaching), the deepest secret of all (सर्व गुह्यतमम्/most confidential); because you are deeply beloved to Me (दृढम् इष्टः असि मे/very dear to Me), therefore I shall speak what is good for you (ते हितम् वक्ष्यामि/will tell your benefit).
Fix your mind on Me (मन्मनाः भव/think of Me), become My devotee (मद्भक्तः/My lover), offer sacrifice to Me (मद्याजी/My worshipper), and bow down to Me (माम् नमस्कुरु/prostrate before Me); you shall surely come to Me alone (माम् एव एष्यसि/will reach Me only), this is My true promise to you (ते सत्यम् प्रतिजाने/truly I promise you) because you are dear to Me (प्रियः असि मे/are loved by Me).
Abandoning all varieties of religion or duties (सर्वधर्मान् परित्यज्य/giving up all dharmas), take refuge in Me alone (माम् एकम् शरणम् व्रज/surrender to Me only); I shall liberate you (त्वा मोक्षयिष्यामि/will free you) from all sins (सर्व पापेभ्यः/all evils), do not grieve (मा शुचः/do not lament).
This teaching should never be spoken by you (ते न वाच्यम्/not to be told by you) to one who is devoid of austerity (अतपस्का/lacking penance), nor to one who is not a devotee (अभक्ता/non-devotee) at any time, nor to one who does not wish to listen (अशुश्रूषू/unwilling to hear), and nor to him who cavils at Me (यः माम् अभ्यसूयति/who envies Me).
He who will declare this supreme secret (परमम् गुह्यम्/highest mystery) among My devotees (मद्भक्त/My followers), having offered supreme devotion to Me (मयि पराम् भक्तिम् कृत्वा/doing high devotion to Me), shall without doubt come to Me alone (माम् एव एष्यति असंशयः/will reach Me without hesitation).
And there is no one among human beings (मनुष्येषु/among men) who does dearer service to Me (प्रियकृत्तमः/most pleasing doer) than he, nor shall there be any other dearer to Me (प्रियतरः/more beloved) than him on earth (भुवि/on world).
And he who will study this righteous dialogue (धर्म्य संवाद/pious conversation) of ours, by him I would be worshipped (इष्टः स्याम्/would be adored) through the sacrifice of knowledge (ज्ञानयज्ञ/wisdom offering); this is My view (मे मतिः/My opinion).
Even the man who merely hears this (शृणुयात् अपि यः नरः/whoever person just listens) with faith (श्रद्धावान्/full of faith) and without envy (अनसूयः/free from malice), he also, being liberated (मुक्त/freed), shall attain the auspicious worlds (शुभान् लोकान्/happy realms) of those who perform virtuous deeds (पुण्यकर्मणाम्/of righteous actors).
Has this been heard by you, O Partha (पार्थ/Arjuna), with a one-pointed mind (एकाग्रेण चेतसा/focused consciousness); has your delusion born of ignorance (अज्ञानसम्मोह/blindness of ignorance) been completely destroyed, O Dhananjaya (धनञ्जय/Arjuna)?
Arjuna said, My delusion is destroyed (नष्टः मोहः/ignorance gone) and memory has been regained (स्मृतिः लब्धा/remembrance obtained) by me through Your grace (त्वत्प्रसादात्/by Your blessing), O infallible one (अच्युत/Krishna); I stand firm (स्थितः अस्मि/am established) with my doubts gone (गतसन्देह/suspicions removed), I will act according to Your word (तव वचनम् करिष्ये/will follow Your instruction).
Sanjaya said, Thus I heard this wonderful (अद्भुतम्/amazing) and hair-raising (रोमहर्षणम्/thrilling) dialogue (संवादम्/conversation) between Vasudeva (वासुदेव/Krishna) and the high-souled Partha (महात्मा पार्थ/noble Arjuna).
By the grace of Vyasa (व्यासप्रसादात्/by Vyasa's blessing) I have heard this supreme secret (परम् गुह्यम्/highest mystery), this yoga (योग/mystic path) directly from Krishna, the Lord of Yoga (योगेश्वर कृष्ण/Master of Yoga) Himself speaking it in person (साक्षात् स्वयम् कथयतः/personally telling).
O King (राजन्/Dhritarashtra), remembering over and over again (संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य/repeatedly recalling) this wonderful and holy dialogue (पुण्य संवाद/pious conversation) between Keshava and Arjuna (केशवार्जुनयोः/Krishna and Arjuna), I rejoice again and again (हृष्यामि च मुहुः मुहुः/feel thrilled repeatedly).
And remembering over and over again (संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य/repeatedly recalling) that most wonderful form (अत्यद्भुतम् रूपम्/extraordinary cosmic shape) of Lord Hari (हरि/Krishna), great is my amazement (महान् विस्मयः/huge wonder), O King (राजन्/Dhritarashtra), and I rejoice again and again (हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः/feel thrilled repeatedly).
Wherever there is Krishna, the Lord of Yoga (योगेश्वरः कृष्णः/Master of Yoga), and wherever there is Partha, the archer (धनुर्धरः पार्थः/bowman Arjuna), there will certainly be fortune (श्रीः/wealth), victory (विजयः/triumph), prosperity (भूतिः/expansion) and firm morality (ध्रुवा नीतिः/lasting ethics); this is my opinion (मम मतिः/my conviction).
